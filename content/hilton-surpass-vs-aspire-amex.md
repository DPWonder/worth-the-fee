For two or three Hilton stays a year, the Surpass ($150) is the easier card to earn back, because its Hilton credits can cover most of the fee on those stays. The Aspire ($550) comes out ahead only if you will use its yearly free night at a hotel you would otherwise pay well for, plus its resort or flight credits.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The two cards side by side

| | Surpass | Aspire |
|---|---|---|
| Annual fee | $150 | $550 |
| Hilton status | Gold | Diamond |
| Points at Hilton | 12 per dollar | 14 per dollar |
| Hilton credit | Up to $50 a quarter ($200 a year), any Hilton property | Up to $200 each half-year ($400 a year), participating Hilton resorts only |
| Free night | Only after $15,000 of purchases in a calendar year | One each year, no spend needed |
| Flight credit | None | Up to $50 a quarter ($200 a year) |
| CLEAR+ credit | None | Up to $219 a year |
| Car rental status | National Emerald Club Executive | National Emerald Club Executive |

Enrollment is needed for some benefits. Unused credits do not carry over to the next quarter or half-year.

## The Surpass sum for two or three stays

The Surpass credit is paid by quarter. Each quarter in which you spend at least $50 directly with a Hilton property gives $50 back.

| Hilton stays, each in a different quarter | Credit used | Fee left to cover |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $50 | $100 |
| 2 | $100 | $50 |
| 3 | $150 | $0 |
| 4 | $200 | $50 ahead |

Two stays in the same quarter count once. Three summer stays in July and August use one $50 credit, not three.

What is left of the fee has to come from Gold status and points. One Mile at a Time lists Gold benefits as breakfast or a daily food and beverage credit, bonus points and room upgrades when available. The free night on this card needs $15,000 of card spending in a calendar year, so an occasional guest who uses the card only at hotels will not earn it.

## The Aspire sum for two or three stays

The Aspire costs $400 more. What it adds for a light traveler:

- **One free night a year.** It arrives in the first card year and after each renewal, and must be used within one year of issue. Its value is the price of the room you use it on.
- **Resort credit, up to $200 per half-year.** It applies only at participating Hilton resorts. A city hotel or a roadside Hampton Inn does not count. Both halves need a resort stay to get the full $400.
- **Flight credit, up to $50 a quarter.** It applies to flights bought from an airline or through Amex Travel. Four separate quarters with a flight purchase are needed for the full $200.
- **CLEAR+ credit, up to $219.** Worth that only if you would pay for CLEAR+.
- **Diamond status** in place of Gold.

Three example years:

| Your year | Aspire value used | Against the $550 fee |
|---|---|---|
| Two city stays, one flight purchase, free night used on a $250 room | $50 + $250 = $300 | $250 short |
| One resort stay, flights in two quarters, free night on a $300 room | $200 + $100 + $300 = $600 | $50 ahead |
| Resort stays in both halves, flights in four quarters, free night on a $400 room | $400 + $200 + $400 = $1,000 | $450 ahead |

The room prices are examples to show the sum, not quotes.

Sources differ on what Hilton points are worth: The Points Guy says 0.4 cents each, One Mile at a Time about 0.5 cents. At two or three stays a year, the two extra points per dollar on the Aspire add only a few dollars.

## How to decide

Ask three questions about the next 12 months:

1. Will at least one stay be at a Hilton resort? If no, the resort credit is worth nothing.
2. Is there a night you would otherwise pay $300 or more for, where you could use the free night?
3. Will you buy airfare in several different quarters?

Two or three "yes" answers point to the Aspire. One or none points to the Surpass.

## Who should skip both

- People whose two or three hotel stays a year are spread across different chains.
- People who stay only at low-priced Hilton brands, where a free night and elite status save little.
- People who do not want to track quarterly and half-year credits.
- People who would put less than $50 a quarter on a Hilton bill.

Welcome offers vary by applicant and change. The Points Guy notes that moving from the Surpass to the Aspire by upgrade may not come with a welcome offer.

## Quick answers

**Does the Surpass come with a free night every year?**
No. The Surpass free night is earned only after $15,000 of purchases in a calendar year. The Aspire gives one each year.

**Can the Aspire resort credit be used at any Hilton?**
No. It applies to charges made directly with participating Hilton resorts, up to $200 in each half of the year.

**Which status does each card give?**
The Surpass gives Hilton Honors Gold. The Aspire gives Diamond.
