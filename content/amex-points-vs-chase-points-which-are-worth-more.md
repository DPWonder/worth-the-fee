Published valuations put the two almost level: The Points Guy values an Amex point at 2.0 cents and a Chase point at 2.05 cents, and One Mile at a Time uses 1.7 cents for both. The real difference is at the bottom and at the edges: Chase points are worth 1 cent as cash against about 0.6 cents for Amex, while Amex has more airline partners and Chase has Hyatt.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## Side by side

| | Amex points (Membership Rewards) | Chase points |
|---|---|---|
| The Points Guy valuation, November 2026 | 2.0 cents | 2.05 cents |
| One Mile at a Time valuation | 1.7 cents | 1.7 cents |
| As cash or statement credit | About 0.6 cents | 1 cent |
| Flights through the card's travel site | 1 cent | 1 cent; up to 2 cents with a Points Boost on a Sapphire Reserve, up to 1.5 or 1.75 on a Sapphire Preferred |
| Most hotels through the travel site | About 0.7 cents | 1 cent; more with a Points Boost |
| Transfer partners | About 20 (16 airline programs, 3 or 4 hotel programs; counts differ) | 16 (12 airline, 4 hotel) |
| Partners at worse than 1 to 1 | Cathay Pacific and Emirates (5 to 4), JetBlue (250 to 200) | Hyatt from a Sapphire Preferred (4 to 3) |
| Fee to transfer | 0.06 cents per point to US airlines, up to $99 | None reported |

The valuations assume transfers to airline and hotel partners and a user who finds good awards. They are estimates, not rates. Details: [Amex point values](/amex-membership-rewards-points-value/) and [Chase Points Boost](/chase-points-boost-explained/).

## Partners only one of them has

| Only Amex | Only Chase | Both |
|---|---|---|
| ANA, Avianca LifeMiles, Delta, Emirates, Qatar Airways, Aeromexico | United, Southwest | Aer Lingus, Air Canada Aeroplan, Flying Blue, British Airways, Iberia, Cathay Pacific, JetBlue, Qantas, Singapore, Virgin Atlantic |
| Hilton, Choice | Hyatt, IHG, Wyndham | Marriott |

Chase added Cathay Pacific and Qantas on November 18, 2026, which narrowed Amex's lead in airline partners. Where both have a partner, Chase's ratio is the same or better: Cathay Pacific and JetBlue are 1 to 1 from Chase and 5 to 4 from Amex.

The values of the partners themselves are in [which Amex partners are worth it](/amex-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/) and [which Chase partners are worth it](/chase-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/).

## Which is worth more, by kind of traveler

**You want cash or simple bookings.** Chase. A point is 1 cent as cash and at least 1 cent on any Chase Travel booking. Amex points drop to 0.6 cents as a statement credit and about 0.7 cents on most Amex Travel hotels.

**You stay at Hyatt.** Chase, with a Sapphire Reserve. The Points Guy values a Hyatt point at 1.65 cents, and the Reserve transfers at 1 to 1. From a Sapphire Preferred the ratio is 4 to 3, which this page works out to about 1.24 cents per Chase point; see [the Hyatt change](/chase-to-hyatt-transfer-change-2026/). Amex's hotel partners are valued at 0.4 to 0.85 cents per partner point.

**You fly United or Southwest.** Chase. Neither is an Amex partner.

**You fly Delta.** Amex. Delta is not a Chase partner. The Points Guy values a SkyMile at 1.2 cents, so this is convenience more than high value.

**You want business class to Japan on ANA.** Amex has the direct route, ANA Mileage Club, at about 100,000 miles round trip in low season. Both programs reach ANA seats through Virgin Atlantic. See [points to Japan](/how-many-points-for-a-flight-to-japan/).

**You want Europe in business class.** Close to a tie. Flying Blue, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia and Aeroplan take both. Amex adds ANA and LifeMiles; Chase adds United. See [points to Europe](/how-many-points-for-a-flight-to-europe/).

## How much the gap matters in dollars

On 100,000 points:

| Use | Amex | Chase |
|---|---|---|
| Cash or statement credit | About $600 | $1,000 |
| Flights through the travel site | $1,000 | $1,000, or up to $2,000 with a Sapphire Reserve Points Boost |
| The Points Guy's valuation | $2,000 | $2,050 |
| One Mile at a Time's valuation | $1,700 | $1,700 |

The $400 gap in the first row is the firm difference. The rest depends on the award you find. The Points Guy also reports that 2-cent Boosts on the Reserve have become harder to find than at launch.

## Where sources disagree

- The Points Guy puts Chase 0.05 cents ahead; One Mile at a Time values the two the same.
- The top Points Boost on a Sapphire Preferred is given as 1.5 cents by some sources and 1.75 cents by others.
- Amex's partner count is 20 or 21 depending on the source.

## Who should skip both

- People who would redeem for cash. A 2% cash-back card pays more than 1 point per dollar at 1 cent.
- People who will not search for award seats. The 1.7 to 2 cent figures only apply to transfers.
- People who close cards often. Both programs can take points back when an account closes; see [whether points expire](/do-amex-and-chase-points-expire/).

## Quick answers

**Which point has the higher cash value?**
Chase, at 1 cent per point. Amex points are worth about 0.6 cents as a statement credit.

**Which has more transfer partners?**
Amex, with about 20 against Chase's 16. Chase is the only one of the two with Hyatt, United and Southwest.

**Are the 2-cent valuations guaranteed?**
No. They are estimates by The Points Guy that assume transfers to partners. One Mile at a Time uses 1.7 cents.
