You see your own Amex Platinum welcome offer by starting an application: Amex shows the points figure before you accept the card, and reviewers report no hard credit check up to that point. People see different numbers because Amex sets the offer for each applicant, so the headline of as high as 175,000 points is a ceiling and not a promise.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## Ways to check, side by side

| Route | What it shows | Credit check | Status in November 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amex public page | Your own offer, after you enter your details | Soft check to show the offer, per The Points Guy | Confirmed |
| A referral link | Your own offer, the same way | Same process reported | Confirmed; can differ from the public route |
| CardMatch tool | Targeted offers from several banks, sometimes Amex | Soft check | Whether it shows a Platinum offer now is not confirmed |
| Mail or email from Amex | A targeted offer, if you get one | None to receive it | Depends on being targeted |
| Private (incognito) window or another browser | The same public page | None to view | Not confirmed to change the offer |
| Prequalification page | Not described in any source read | Not confirmed | Not confirmed |

## The Amex application

The Points Guy describes the process this way: you submit an application, Amex generally runs a soft credit check, and your own welcome offer appears. You then accept or decline.

- If you decline, One Mile at a Time and Upgraded Points both report no effect on your credit score.
- If you accept, Amex may run a hard inquiry and opens the account.

Write down the points figure and the spending requirement from the offer screen, or save a copy of it.

A referral link leads to the same kind of application, and its number can match, beat or trail the public route. See [referral against public offer](/amex-platinum-referral-vs-public-offer/).

## CardMatch

CardMatch is a third-party tool that asks for your name, address and the last four digits of your Social Security number, then lists card offers you may be matched with. One Mile at a Time reports it uses only a soft check.

Its limits: that page was last updated in 2022, when it showed no targeted Platinum offer. No source read confirms a Platinum offer in CardMatch this month.

## Mail and email offers

Upgraded Points and The Points Guy both suggest opting in to Amex marketing messages, in your Amex account settings, to have a chance at a targeted offer. One Mile at a Time calls larger targeted offers rare. You cannot request one.

## Private windows and other browsers

This is the least certain route. Frequent Miler wrote in 2025 that some referral links showed the top offer only when opened "in certain ways or in certain browsers."

No 2026 article read for this page confirms that a private window changes the Platinum offer. Since Amex now sets the figure after you enter your details, the browser may matter less than it once did. Treat it as unproven.

## Why the numbers differ

- **Amex sets the offer per applicant.** Amex does not publish how. Upgraded Points names credit score, income and existing Amex cards as likely factors; that is the site's reading, not Amex's statement.
- **Your history with the card.** If you have or had the Platinum Card, you may be shown no welcome offer at all. See [the once-per-lifetime rule](/amex-once-per-lifetime-welcome-offer-rule/).
- **The route.** Referral links and targeted mailers can carry different offers from the public page.
- **The date.** Offers change. The spending requirement rose from $8,000 to $12,000 in February 2026.

## Who should skip it

- Anyone who has held the Platinum Card before. Checking is harmless, but the likely answer is no offer.
- Anyone who cannot reach $12,000 of normal spending in six months. The points figure does not matter if the requirement is out of reach. See [what counts toward it](/amex-platinum-welcome-offer-spending-requirement/).
- Anyone planning a mortgage or car loan soon. Viewing an offer is reported as a soft check, but accepting adds a hard inquiry and a new account. See [how an application affects your score](/does-applying-for-a-travel-card-hurt-credit-score/).
- Anyone who would not keep the card at $895 a year without the welcome offer.

## Quick answers

**Can I see my Amex Platinum offer without a hard credit check?**
Reviewers report yes. Amex shows the offer after a soft check, and a hard inquiry follows only if you accept.

**Does a private browser window show a higher offer?**
Not confirmed. Older reports say browsers sometimes mattered for referral links. No 2026 source read confirms it for the Platinum Card.

**Why did someone else get a higher number than I did?**
Amex sets each applicant's offer. Card history, credit profile, the route used and the date can all differ.
