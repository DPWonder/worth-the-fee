Generally, a welcome bonus you earn by meeting a spending requirement is treated as a rebate on your purchases and not as taxable income. A referral bonus, which you get without spending, is generally treated as taxable income, and the card issuer may report it to the IRS on Form 1099-MISC. This page states what sources say and is not tax advice.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The general rule, by type of reward

| Reward | Tied to spending? | How sources describe the tax treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Points or cash back on purchases | Yes | Rebate, generally not income |
| Welcome bonus after a spending requirement | Yes | Rebate, generally not income |
| Referral bonus for referring a friend | No | Generally taxable income |
| Bonus for opening a bank account | No | Generally taxable income |

The Points Guy's tax guide puts the line at spending: rewards that need a purchase are generally not income, and bonuses received without spending are. Thrifty Traveler describes a welcome bonus tied to a spending requirement as "a rebate on spending rather than income."

The person who is referred does not owe tax on a welcome bonus they earn by spending, according to The Points Guy. The tax question sits with the person who made the referral.

## Where the rebate idea comes from

The IRS has long treated rewards earned on purchases as a reduction in the price paid. The Points Guy cites a 2010 IRS memorandum for cash back.

One court case marks the edge. In Anikeev v. Commissioner (US Tax Court, February 2021), rewards earned by buying money orders and reloading prepaid debit cards were held taxable, because those are cash equivalents and not products or services. Rewards on gift card purchases in the same case were not. The Points Guy's summary says ordinary purchases are not affected.

## Referral bonuses and Form 1099-MISC

| Item | What sources say |
|---|---|
| Amex | Sends Form 1099-MISC for referral bonuses, and counts each Membership Rewards point as 1 cent (AwardWallet, Thrifty Traveler) |
| Chase | Has sent 1099s for referral points, valued at 1 cent each (One Mile at a Time, 2020). A search summary of Chase's referral terms says the bonus may be taxable income to you |
| Where it goes on a return | Other income, Schedule 1, line 8z (The Points Guy) |
| No form received | The income is still reportable (The Points Guy, Thrifty Traveler) |

At 1 cent a point, 50,000 referral points would be reported as $500 of income.

## The reporting threshold changed for 2026

| Payments made in | Form 1099-MISC box 3 threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 and earlier | $600 | The Points Guy, Doctor of Credit |
| 2026 | $2,000 | IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC |
| 2027 on | $2,000, may be adjusted for inflation | IRS instructions |

The threshold decides when an issuer must send a form. It does not decide whether the income is taxable. Referral bonuses under the threshold are still income under the general rule above.

Some guides written early in 2026 still give $600, because they describe 2025 tax returns. Whether Amex and Chase will send forms only at $2,000 for 2026, or below it, is not confirmed.

## What this means for someone about to apply

- A welcome bonus earned by spending on a personal card is generally not something you report.
- If you later refer friends, the points you earn are generally income at about 1 cent each, in the year you receive them.
- Keep your own record of any referral points and the dates they posted.

State tax treatment was not researched for this page and is not confirmed.

## Who should skip relying on this page

- Anyone who earned referral or bank account bonuses this year. Check your own figures with a tax professional or the IRS instructions.
- Anyone meeting spending requirements with money orders, prepaid card reloads or similar cash equivalents. The Anikeev case treated those rewards as taxable, and card issuers generally do not count such transactions anyway.
- Anyone who received a 1099 that looks wrong. Ask the issuer what the form covers before you file.

## Quick answers

**Do I pay tax on a credit card welcome bonus?**
Generally no, when you earned it by meeting a spending requirement. Sources describe it as a rebate on purchases.

**Are referral bonuses taxable?**
Generally yes. They are not tied to spending, and issuers such as Amex report them on Form 1099-MISC at 1 cent a point.

**What is the 1099-MISC threshold for 2026?**
The IRS instructions set it at $2,000 for tax years beginning after 2025. It was $600 before.
