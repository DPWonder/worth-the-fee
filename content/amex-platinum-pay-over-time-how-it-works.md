The Platinum Card works like a charge card: it has no preset spending limit, and charges are due in full each month unless a feature called Pay Over Time is active. With Pay Over Time active, eligible charges can be carried as a balance with interest, up to a limit Amex sets, which makes that part of the account behave like a credit card.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## Two balances on one account

The facts on this page come from Amex's Card Member Agreement for the Platinum Card, with a rates table dated September 18, 2025. Your own agreement may show different figures.

The agreement describes two balances:

| Balance | What goes in it | When it is due |
|---|---|---|
| Pay In Full | Charges made while Pay Over Time is inactive, and charges that do not fit under the Pay Over Time Limit | In full, every month |
| Pay Over Time | Eligible charges made while Pay Over Time is active | At least the minimum each month; the rest can be carried with interest |

A traditional charge card has only the first balance. This account can have both.

## How Pay Over Time works

The agreement says: "Pay Over Time has two settings: active and inactive."

- **Active.** Eligible charges are added to the Pay Over Time balance automatically, up to your Pay Over Time Limit. An eligible annual fee can be added too. Each month you may pay the full new balance, the minimum, or any amount between.
- **Inactive.** New purchases and the annual fee go to the Pay In Full balance and are due in full.
- **Changing it.** You can view or change the setting in your online account or by phone. The Points Guy says a change takes effect right away.

Cash advances are not eligible. Amex can suspend the feature based on its view of your credit or the state of the account.

Whether Pay Over Time is active by default on a new Platinum account is not confirmed. Check the setting after the card arrives.

## The Pay Over Time Limit is not a spending limit

The card has no preset spending limit. The agreement says Amex decides whether to approve each charge from your spending and payment record, your credit history and the resources it knows about. A large charge can still be declined.

The Pay Over Time Limit is a separate number. It caps how much you can carry with interest, not how much you can spend. Charges above it are still possible, but they are due in full.

## What it costs

| Item | Amount in the September 2025 agreement |
|---|---|
| APR for Pay Over Time | Prime Rate + 12.74% to Prime Rate + 21.74%, variable |
| Penalty APR | Prime Rate + 26.74%, for at least 6 months after a late or returned payment |
| Minimum due on a Pay Over Time balance | The highest of $40, 2% of the balance, or a tiered formula |
| Late payment fee | Up to $40 |
| Returned payment fee | Up to $40 |
| Annual fee | $895 |

The Prime Rate moves, so the percentage on your statement changes with it.

### How to pay no interest

The agreement says Amex will not charge interest on charges added automatically to Pay Over Time if you pay the Account Total New Balance by the due date each month. The due date is at least 25 days after the billing period closes.

So having Pay Over Time active costs nothing by itself. Interest starts only when you pay less than the full balance.

## Plan It

Plan It is a separate tool on the same account. You split a qualifying purchase into a set number of monthly payments with a fixed monthly fee in place of interest. The agreement caps the fee at 1.33% of the purchase per month, and the fee is shown before you confirm. The minimum purchase size is not stated in the agreement; The Points Guy gives it as over $100.

## Credit reports

The Points Guy says the Pay Over Time Limit is not reported to the credit bureaus. The agreement says Amex does report account information, including a failure to pay as agreed.

## Who should skip it

Pay Over Time is a costly way to borrow. Leave it inactive, or do not rely on it, if:

- You expect to carry a balance often. Compare the rate in your agreement with other ways to borrow first.
- A due-in-full bill in a heavy spending month would be hard to pay.
- You want a fixed, known credit line for planning.

People who pay in full every month are not affected by the rate. For them, the card's cost is the annual fee; see [what the credits are worth](/amex-platinum-credits-real-value/).

## Quick answers

**Is the Amex Platinum a charge card?**
It works like one. Charges are due in full each month unless Pay Over Time is active for eligible charges.

**Does Pay Over Time charge interest if the bill is paid in full?**
No. The agreement says no interest is charged on automatically added charges when the full new balance is paid by the due date each month.

**Can Pay Over Time be turned off?**
Yes. The setting can be changed to inactive online or by phone, and new purchases are then due in full.
