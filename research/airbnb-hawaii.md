# Fact sheet: Airbnb hosting in Hawaii

Checked 2026-11-20. Each line ends with the source it came from. "Opened" means the page was fetched and read; "search result" means the fact was seen in a search result summary of that URL and the page itself was not opened (or could not be: several .gov pages returned 403/404 to automated fetches). UNCONFIRMED marks anything not verified on a primary source.

## 1. County rules

### Honolulu (Oahu)

- A short-term rental (STR) is lodging for less than 30 consecutive days; the Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP) enforces the 30-day line. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/str/
- Two types: bed and breakfast homes (owner or permanent resident present, up to two rooms) and transient vacation units (whole-home, unhosted). (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/str/
- STRs are permitted only in resort-zoned areas "and certain apartment-zoned areas"; maps are in the Land Use Ordinance amendments (the page cites Ordinance 25-52) and there is an STR eligibility map. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/str/
- SOURCES DISAGREE (both are DPP pages): the DPP FAQ says STRs are permitted "only in resort-zoned areas" under Ordinance 25-52, with no mention of apartment zones. Treat the eligibility map or a written zoning verification as the answer. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/str/str-faq/
- The STR must be registered with DPP (through HNL Build); each unit is registered separately; registration lasts one year and is renewed no earlier than three months and no later than one month before it expires; a change of owner or operator needs a new registration. Hotel and timeshare units are excluded from registration. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/str/str-faq/
- Nonconforming use certificates (NUCs) cover units operating since before October 22, 1986; no new NUCs are issued; renewal runs September 1 to October 15 each year; NUC holders do not need to register. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/str/
- Advertising: an ad for stays under 30 days must show the STR registration or NUC number and the tax map key (TMK). An unregistered, non-NUC listing must state: "this property may not be rented for less than 30 consecutive days." (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/str/str-faq/
- Zoning verification: written request, $300, or call (808) 768-8015; STR contact STR@honolulu.gov, 768-7887. (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/permitting/str/str-faq/
- Sellers must give buyers a short-term rental disclosure form saying whether the property may legally be used as an STR (Ordinance 22-6). (opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/dpp/str/
- The 90-day rule: Ordinance 22-7 (Bill 41, 2022) tried to raise the minimum rental outside resort areas from 30 to 90 days. A federal judge blocked enforcement for 30-to-89-day rentals in October 2022 and later made the injunction permanent; reports describe the permanent ruling as protecting rentals that were already operating. (search results) https://www.courthousenews.com/existing-short-term-hawaii-rentals-may-remain-in-operation-judge-rules/ and https://www.avalara.com/mylodgetax/en/blog/2024/01/judge-rules-honolulu-short-term-rentals-can-continue-operating.html
- SOURCES DISAGREE: Airbnb's own Honolulu help page still says under Bill 41 "stays under 90 days will not be permitted in most zones", while the DPP pages opened today use 30 days throughout and do not mention 90 days. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/894
- UNCONFIRMED: whether a brand-new 30-to-89-day rental (one not operating before the 2022 ordinance) is protected by the court ruling. Not stated on the DPP pages.
- UNCONFIRMED: registration fee. A third-party guide gives $1,000 initial and $500 renewal; the DPP pages opened do not list fees. (search result) https://www.rentcompliant.com/guides/hawaii/honolulu/

### Maui County

- Bill 9 passed the County Council 5-3 and Mayor Richard Bissen signed it on December 15, 2025. It phases out transient vacation rentals in apartment-zoned districts (the "Minatoya list"), about 7,000 units. (opened) https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2025-12-16/maui-county-passes-historic-law-to-phase-out-thousands-of-vacation-rentals
- Deadlines: January 1, 2029 for West Maui; January 1, 2031 for the rest of the county. (opened) https://mauinow.com/2026/01/02/as-expected-bill-9-challenged-in-court-lawsuits-seek-to-block-short-term-rental-phaseout/
- Bill 9 is Ordinance 5909; it covers the A-1 and A-2 apartment districts; last legal guest night is December 31, 2028 (West Maui) and December 31, 2030 (elsewhere). (opened; real-estate site, updated November 6, 2026) https://www.mauiproperty.com/bill-9-maui-update-2026-deadlines-minatoya-list-info/
- Until those dates, Minatoya-list condos may still operate. Not affected by Bill 9: hotel- and resort-zoned properties, permitted bed and breakfasts and short-term rental homes with valid county permits; timeshares are not affected. (opened) https://www.mauiproperty.com/bill-9-maui-update-2026-deadlines-minatoya-list-info/ and https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2025-12-16/maui-county-passes-historic-law-to-phase-out-thousands-of-vacation-rentals
- Lawsuits (both in 2nd Circuit Court, state): Malter v. Maui County, 2CCV-25-0003778, filed December 19, 2025 by Kaanapali Royal owners, seeking a preliminary injunction; Lynam v. County of Maui, 2CCV-25-0003780, filed December 22, 2025, a proposed class action for all Minatoya owners. Both claim an unconstitutional taking of vested rights. (opened) https://mauinow.com/2026/01/02/as-expected-bill-9-challenged-in-court-lawsuits-seek-to-block-short-term-rental-phaseout/
- As of early November 2026 no court has issued an injunction, so the deadlines stand. (opened; real-estate site) https://www.mauiproperty.com/bill-9-maui-update-2026-deadlines-minatoya-list-info/
- UNCONFIRMED: no news-outlet or court source dated after January 2026 was found for the lawsuits. The "no injunction" status rests on one real-estate site.
- Rezoning route: the Maui Planning Commission voted in February 2026 to deny new H-3 and H-4 hotel districts; its vote is advisory. (search result) https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/02/25/maui-planning-commission-denies-new-hotel-districts-short-term-rentals/
- The Council's Housing and Land Use Committee advanced the hotel-zoning bill 6-1 in May 2026. (search result) https://mauinow.com/2026/05/27/new-hotel-zoning-clears-council-committee-6-1-despite-planning-commissions-opposition/
- Bill 88 (Ordinance 6008) created the H-3 and H-4 districts and took effect June 22, 2026. It rezones nothing by itself; each property needs its own rezoning. (opened; real-estate site) https://www.mauiproperty.com/bill-9-maui-update-2026-deadlines-minatoya-list-info/
- SOURCES DISAGREE on small points: the final Bill 88 vote is given as June 19 (7-2) by one source and adoption as June 22 by another. (search results) https://mauirealestateadvisors.com/bill-9-implementation-update-bill-88-passes-final-vote-7-2-heads-to-mayor-for-signature/
- Rezoning status reported as of November 6, 2026: Planning Commission recommended approval for 5 of 48 properties in the first group on September 22, 2026 and denial for 43; the Council sent 32 more complexes for review on September 29, 2026; two resolutions failed 4-5 on October 23, 2026; a rezoning the commission opposes needs six of nine Council votes; a new Council takes office in January 2027. No Minatoya property had completed rezoning in the sources read. (opened; real-estate site) https://www.mauiproperty.com/bill-9-maui-update-2026-deadlines-minatoya-list-info/
- UNCONFIRMED: Maui County's own Bill 9 page (https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/18061) returned 403 and could not be read.
- Airbnb listings in Maui County carry fields for the TMK and the state TAT number; listings without a valid TMK can be removed. (search result) https://news.airbnb.com/an-update-on-our-work-to-support-responsible-hosting-in-maui

### Kauai

- Outside the Visitor Destination Area (VDA), any short-term rental of a home or a room for less than 180 days is not permitted and cannot be applied for. The limit does not apply inside the VDA. (opened) https://www.kauai.gov/Government/Departments-Agencies/Planning/Transient-Vacation-Rentals
- Outside the VDA only properties already determined to be non-conforming (holding a non-conforming use certificate) or approved homestays may operate. The county publishes the list by TMK; a property not on it should not be operating. (opened) https://www.kauai.gov/Government/Departments-Agencies/Planning/Transient-Vacation-Rentals
- Certificate renewal: yearly, by certified mail at least two months before the renewal date; no grace period; a late filer gets a cease-and-desist and forfeiture and cannot reapply (Ordinance 950). After a sale the new owner must file the current-year renewal within 30 days of recordation. (opened) https://www.kauai.gov/Government/Departments-Agencies/Planning/Transient-Vacation-Rentals
- Renewal needs current GET and TAT certificates; the 2026 form says it is unlawful to run short-term rentals outside the VDA without a permit; Planning Department (808) 241-4050. (search result) https://www.kauai.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/planning-department/documents/tvr/2026renewalapplicationtvrfillable.pdf
- UNCONFIRMED: which towns are inside the VDA. Town-level claims came only from real-estate blogs; check the parcel on the county map.
- UNCONFIRMED: current certificate renewal fee (a 2023 form showed $750).

### Hawaii County (Big Island)

- Existing zoning rule (Bill 108, Ordinance 2018-114): unhosted short-term vacation rentals are allowed in the V (resort), CG and CV districts and in residential and commercial districts inside General Plan Resort and Resort Node areas; elsewhere a rental needs a nonconforming use certificate for a use that existed before the ordinance. County code sections 25-4-16 and 25-4-16.1. (search result of county documents; the wording seen was a draft) https://planning.hawaiicounty.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/301489/637203350585600000
- New registration law: Bill 47 became Ordinance 25-50 (2025). It requires registration of both hosted and unhosted transient vacation rentals (rentals of 180 days or less), is registration only, and does not change zoning. Fees: $250 hosted, $500 unhosted. Fine for not registering: up to $10,000. (opened) https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2026-01-06/rules-fees-hawaii-island-vacation-rentals
- Start date moved twice: December 20, 2025, then July 1, 2026. (opened) https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2026-01-06/rules-fees-hawaii-island-vacation-rentals
- Registration opened September 1, 2026 through an online portal, and Bill 175 keeps the registration period open to the end of 2026. (opened; article dated September 8, 2026) https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=377152
- "Hawaii County TVR registration is now live" (site run by two council members, not an official county page). (opened) https://www.hawaiicountytar.com/
- Pending, not law in the sources read: Bill 147 would rename hosted rentals "bed and breakfast" and unhosted ones "short-term vacation rental", widen the zones where unhosted rentals are allowed, and add management standards and fines. It was deferred to a September 15, 2026 committee meeting. (opened) https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/?p=377152
- UNCONFIRMED: Bill 147's status after September 2026; whether a registration number must appear in ads; the county Planning page (https://www.planning.hawaiicounty.gov/resources/short-term-vacation-rentals) returned 403.

## 2. Taxes

- State Transient Accommodations Tax (TAT): 11% from January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2030, up from 10.25%, under Act 96 of 2025 (the climate "green fee"). (opened, PDF) https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/news/announce/ann26-01.pdf
- The rate applies whether or not the host passes the tax on to the guest. (opened, PDF) https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/news/announce/ann25-03.pdf
- Only the cruise-ship part of Act 96 was enjoined; the 11% rate for lodging is in force. (opened, PDF) https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/news/announce/ann26-01.pdf
- TAT applies to accommodation rented to a transient for less than 180 consecutive days. (opened, PDF, revised May 2025) https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/legal/brochures/TAT_brochure-25.pdf
- County TAT: state law caps each county's rate at 3%. (search result) https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0237D/HRS_0237D-0002_0005.htm
- Kauai County TAT: 3%, since October 1, 2021; due the 20th of the following month (monthly filers) or the 20th after quarter end; annual reconciliation; paid to the county separately using the state TAT number. (opened) https://www.kauai.gov/Government/Departments-Agencies/Finance/Transient-Accommodations-Tax
- Maui County TAT: 3%, since November 1, 2021; no separate county registration; payment is based on proceeds reported to the state. (search result) https://www.mauicounty.gov/3089/Maui-County-Transient-Accommodations-Tax
- Honolulu (Oahu) TAT: 3%, effective December 14, 2021 (Ordinance 21-33); owed by anyone who pays state TAT on Oahu property; the city began mailing assessment notices for unpaid 2023 tax in May 2026. (rate from search result; effective date and notices opened) https://www.honolulu.gov/bfs/otat/
- Hawaii County TAT: UNCONFIRMED on an official page (https://hawaiicounty.gov/departments/finance/transient-accommodations-tax-tat returned 403). News reports say all four counties charge 3%, for 14% combined. (search result) https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/hawaii-green-tax-approved
- General Excise Tax (GET): 4% state plus a 0.5% county surcharge in all four counties (Honolulu, Kauai, Hawaii, Maui) through December 31, 2030, so 4.5%. If passed on to the customer, the maximum pass-on rate is 4.712%. (opened) https://tax.hawaii.gov/?p=7651
- GET and TAT are both owed on gross rents from transient accommodations. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/894
- Registration numbers: a host needs a state GET licence and a TAT certificate of registration. State law requires any ad, including an online one, to show the TAT registration number or a link to it; the fine for a first citation can be $5,000. (search results) https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/legal/taxfacts/tf2017-2.pdf and https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/2021/title-14/chapter-237d/section-237d-4/
- Airbnb tells Honolulu hosts to add their TMK, TAT and registration numbers to the listing. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/894
- Does Airbnb collect and remit in Hawaii? Hawaii is not on Airbnb's list of areas where it collects and remits tax (the US list runs Alabama to Wyoming without Hawaii). The host is responsible. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2509
- A 2026 state bill (HB1590) would have made platforms tax collection agents from January 1, 2027. One secondary source says it failed; no Act was found. UNCONFIRMED on the legislature's site (403). (search result) https://www.bookwithhaven.com/blog/hawaii-short-term-rental-laws
- UNCONFIRMED: state filing frequency thresholds for GET and TAT (not read on a state page).
- Maui County property tax class for short-term rentals, fiscal 2026-2027: $13.00 per $1,000 of assessed value up to $900,000, $15.00 to $3,000,000, $17.00 above. (opened; broker blog dated November 20, 2026) https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/front-desk-independent-manager-or-self-managed-how-to-rent-out-a-maui-condo-in-2026/

## 3. Airbnb host service fee, 2026

- Airbnb is moving all home hosts to a single (host-only) fee: "Most hosts pay 15.5%", the rest typically 14% to 16%. The whole fee comes out of the host's payout. It is mandatory for most hosts, including those using property management software, and a host who has switched cannot switch back. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857
- The older split fee (most hosts 3%, guests 14.1% to 16.5%) is being phased out. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857
- UNCONFIRMED: whether a given Hawaii host has already been moved; Airbnb gives no date for the end of the split fee. Also not confirmed: whether the fee is calculated on tax amounts a host adds to the price.

## 4. Airbnb "refer a host" programme

- Rewards shown on the referral page: "$105 to $1,000 USD for homes in the United States", $50 for experiences, $100 to $400 for services; eligible locations and listing types only; these amounts expire January 20, 2027. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/refer
- Referred host must be a new, first-time host who publishes an entire-home listing (or an experience or service) for the first time. Current or former hosts do not qualify. Private rooms, shared rooms, hotel rooms and listings made through API-connected software are not eligible unless stated. (opened; terms last updated November 11, 2026) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3857
- What the new host must do: complete a first booking within 180 days of starting the listing from the referral link; for homes the booking must be worth at least $100 before taxes and fees, be paid through Airbnb, not cancelled, and the stay completed. Bookings by the host's family or friends do not count. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3857
- The reward goes to the referrer, within 30 days of the qualifying booking per the terms (about 14 days per the overview), capped at the first 25 referred home hosts. Referral links are for personal, non-commercial use and "should not be posted on commercial websites or social media unless Airbnb specifies otherwise"; paid advertising of the link is not allowed. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3857 and https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3405
- Does the new host get anything? The standard programme pages describe no reward for the new host. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3405
- Article 4129 is a separate, ended campaign for service hosts (title names Bali and Tokyo): $200 to the referrer and $100 to the new host, for listings submitted February 15 to April 30, 2026. It does not cover Hawaii homes. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4129
- Is Hawaii eligible? UNCONFIRMED. Article 3858 (effective September 9, 2026) holds the eligible-location lists but the home list did not load in the fetched page, and neither "Hawaii" nor any state exclusion appears on any page read. The referral page says home rewards apply to "homes in the United States". (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3858
- Note for the site: the programme terms restrict posting referral links on commercial websites. (opened) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3857

## 5. Typical costs

- Resort front-desk rental programmes on Maui "typically run 35 to 60 percent of gross rental revenue"; no figure given for independent managers. (opened; broker blog) https://www.hawaiilife.com/blog/front-desk-independent-manager-or-self-managed-how-to-rent-out-a-maui-condo-in-2026/
- One Maui manager's published rates: 20% full service, 10% partner. Single data point. (search result) https://onefinebnb.com/maui-condo-home-review
- Cleaning: one Wailea condo programme lists $264 per stay for a one-bedroom and $314 for a two-bedroom, plus tax (undated; page returned 403 when opened). (search result) https://hawaiivacationcondos.outrigger.com/hawaii/maui/palms-at-wailea/faq/cleaning-fees-damage-protection
- UNCONFIRMED: any statewide average for cleaning or management cost. None was found from a credible survey.
