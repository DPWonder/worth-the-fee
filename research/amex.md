## 6. Platinum Card: welcome offer and eligibility

- Wording now: "As High As 175,000 points. Find Out Your Offer." New cardmembers "may be eligible for as high as 175,000" points after spending $12,000 in the first six months. Offers vary; the top number is not guaranteed. https://thepointsguy.com/credit-cards/reviews/amex-platinum-review/
- Same wording and spend ($12,000 in six months), dated 2026-11-10. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- How it works: you apply, Amex shows your offer amount, then you choose to accept or not; "there won't be a hard pull unless you accept the offer". Declining is described as having no effect on your credit score. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- UNCONFIRMED caution: reader comments on that page claim some applicants saw an offer of about 80,000 points only after a hard pull. Not verified by the article. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- Lifetime rule: one welcome offer per card, generally once in a lifetime. People who have or had this card (or earlier versions) are generally not eligible. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- Amex may show a pop-up during the application telling you that you are not eligible for the offer. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- Upgrading or changing from another Amex card generally does not earn the welcome offer; an authorized user on someone else's Platinum can still get the offer on their own account. https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
- Some targeted invitations have no lifetime wording (invitation only). https://onemileatatime.com/reviews/credit-cards/american-express/amex-platinum-card-bonus/
