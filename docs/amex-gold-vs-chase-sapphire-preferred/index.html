Amex Gold or Chase Sapphire Preferred for someone who spends most on food?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Amex Gold fits a food spender who buys a lot at US supermarkets, because it pays 4 points per dollar there and the Sapphire Preferred pays 1. For someone whose food spending is mostly restaurants, the gap is 1 point per dollar, and the $95 Preferred usually costs less overall.

Superstores and warehouse clubs do not count as supermarkets on the Gold. The Gold's credits all need enrollment and run monthly or by half-year.

The plain sum

The fee gap is $230. The Gold has to return that much more than the Preferred, in points or in credits a reader would have paid cash for.

Extra points on the Gold: 1 per dollar at restaurants, 3 per dollar at supermarkets.

What a point is worth depends on how it is used. At 1 cent, 24,000 extra points are $240. One Mile at a Time uses 1.7 cents for points moved to airline partners, which makes them about $408. The Points Guy uses about 2 cents.

Read against the $230 gap:

  • Restaurants only. 6,000 extra points are worth $60 to $120. The Gold needs about $110 to $170 of its credits, used at face value, to draw level.
  • Restaurants and supermarkets, $12,000 together. The extra points cover the gap by themselves at 1 cent each.
  • Family grocery bill of $10,000 or more. The Gold is well ahead before any credit is counted.

Someone who already orders from Uber Eats monthly and eats at a Resy restaurant twice a year uses $220 with no change in habits. Someone who uses none of these places should count them as zero. The page on whether the Gold is worth its fee has more on each credit.

What the Preferred does that the Gold does not

  • 3x on gas, EV charging and selected streaming.
  • Trip cancellation cover up to $10,000 per traveler and primary rental car cover.
  • Transfers to United, Southwest and Hyatt (Hyatt at 4 Chase points to 3).
  • A fee that is $230 lower every year, with nothing to track.

Which pattern favors which card

  • Cooks at home, large supermarket bill: Amex Gold.
  • Eats out often, small grocery bill, uses Uber and Resy anyway: close; the Gold's credits decide it.
  • Eats out often, uses none of the Gold's credit partners: Sapphire Preferred.
  • Buys groceries at Walmart, Target or Costco: neither card pays a bonus there, so the lower fee wins: Sapphire Preferred.
  • Flies Delta on points: Amex Gold. Stays at Hyatt or flies United or Southwest on points: Sapphire Preferred.

Who should skip both

  • Anyone who carries a balance month to month.
  • Anyone who wants plain cash back and no points to manage; see Blue Cash Preferred or Everyday.
  • Anyone with a small food budget. With little spending in the bonus categories, neither fee is covered by points.

Welcome offers on both cards vary by applicant and change. Amex shows its offer before a decision is final; Chase limits each Sapphire bonus to once per person.

Quick answers

Which card earns more at restaurants? The Gold, 4 points per dollar against 3. On $6,000 a year that is 6,000 more points.

Does the Sapphire Preferred earn a bonus at the supermarket? Only on online grocery orders, at 3x. In-store supermarket purchases earn 1x.

Do either of these cards get me into airport lounges? No. Neither card includes lounge access.
