Are Amex points or Chase points worth more?

Facts checked November 20, 20265 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Published valuations put the two almost level: The Points Guy values an Amex point at 2.0 cents and a Chase point at 2.05 cents, and One Mile at a Time uses 1.7 cents for both. The real difference is at the bottom and at the edges: Chase points are worth 1 cent as cash against about 0.6 cents for Amex, while Amex has more airline partners and Chase has Hyatt.

Which is worth more, by kind of traveler

You want cash or simple bookings. Chase. A point is 1 cent as cash and at least 1 cent on any Chase Travel booking. Amex points drop to 0.6 cents as a statement credit and about 0.7 cents on most Amex Travel hotels.

You stay at Hyatt. Chase, with a Sapphire Reserve. The Points Guy values a Hyatt point at 1.65 cents, and the Reserve transfers at 1 to 1. From a Sapphire Preferred the ratio is 4 to 3, which this page works out to about 1.24 cents per Chase point; see the Hyatt change. Amex's hotel partners are valued at 0.4 to 0.85 cents per partner point.

You fly United or Southwest. Chase. Neither is an Amex partner.

You fly Delta. Amex. Delta is not a Chase partner. The Points Guy values a SkyMile at 1.2 cents, so this is convenience more than high value.

You want business class to Japan on ANA. Amex has the direct route, ANA Mileage Club, at about 100,000 miles round trip in low season. Both programs reach ANA seats through Virgin Atlantic. See points to Japan.

You want Europe in business class. Close to a tie. Flying Blue, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia and Aeroplan take both. Amex adds ANA and LifeMiles; Chase adds United. See points to Europe.

Where sources disagree

  • The Points Guy puts Chase 0.05 cents ahead; One Mile at a Time values the two the same.
  • The top Points Boost on a Sapphire Preferred is given as 1.5 cents by some sources and 1.75 cents by others.
  • Amex's partner count is 20 or 21 depending on the source.

Who should skip both

  • People who would redeem for cash. A 2% cash-back card pays more than 1 point per dollar at 1 cent.
  • People who will not search for award seats. The 1.7 to 2 cent figures only apply to transfers.
  • People who close cards often. Both programs can take points back when an account closes; see whether points expire.

Quick answers

Which point has the higher cash value? Chase, at 1 cent per point. Amex points are worth about 0.6 cents as a statement credit.

Which has more transfer partners? Amex, with about 20 against Chase's 16. Chase is the only one of the two with Hyatt, United and Southwest.

Are the 2-cent valuations guaranteed? No. They are estimates by The Points Guy that assume transfers to partners. One Mile at a Time uses 1.7 cents.
