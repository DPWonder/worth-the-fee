Chase Sapphire Reserve or Capital One Venture X: which premium card costs less to keep?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Venture X costs less to keep. After its $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles its $395 fee nets to about zero, while the Sapphire Reserve's $795 fee nets to $495 after its $300 travel credit, and the rest has to come from narrower credits.

Credit amounts are "up to" figures. The Reserve's dining and StubHub credits are split by half-year, and its Lyft and Peloton credits are monthly.

The cost to keep each card

Venture X: $395, minus $300 of travel booked through Capital One Travel, minus 10,000 miles worth $100 against travel. That is minus $5 from the second year. The miles arrive at the first anniversary.

Sapphire Reserve: $795 minus $300 is $495. The travel credit is the easy one, since it applies to travel purchases in general, with no portal needed. To clear the other $495 a reader needs some of these, at prices they would have paid anyway:

Where the Reserve earns the difference back

  • Guests in lounges. The Reserve admits two guests free. Since February 1, 2026 the Venture X charges for each one. A couple with one child making four lounge visits a year pays about $280 in guest fees on the Venture X: $35 per guest at Priority Pass lounges, or $45 per adult and $25 per child at Capital One's own.
  • Travel booked direct. $10,000 of flights and hotels booked with the airline or hotel earns 40,000 points on the Reserve and 20,000 miles on the Venture X.
  • Hyatt, United and Southwest. The Reserve transfers to all three at 1 to 1. Capital One miles go to none of them.
  • Points Boost. Reserve points can be worth up to 2 cents on selected Chase Travel bookings. See how Points Boost works.

Where the Venture X stays ahead

  • General spending. $30,000 of purchases outside travel and dining earns 60,000 miles on the Venture X and 30,000 points on the Reserve.
  • A solo traveler. No guest fees to pay, and the same Priority Pass network.
  • Low effort. One portal booking a year covers the fee. Nothing is monthly.

Which pattern favors which card

  • One traveler, a few trips, mixed spending: Venture X.
  • Family or couple using lounges together: Sapphire Reserve, if the guest fees and credits used add up to more than the $400 fee gap.
  • Hyatt stays on points, or heavy direct airfare: Sapphire Reserve.
  • Would not change where they eat or stay to use credits: Venture X.

Who should skip both

  • Anyone who carries a balance.
  • Anyone who visits a lounge once or twice a year. A $95 card costs less; see Preferred or Reserve for a light traveler.
  • For the Venture X: anyone unwilling to book through a card portal, since both of its fee offsets depend on travel.
  • For the Reserve: anyone with five or more new personal cards in 24 months, whom Chase generally declines.

Welcome offers on both cards vary and change, so they are not in these sums.

Quick answers

Which card is cheaper after the travel credit? The Venture X. It nets to about zero with the anniversary miles; the Reserve nets to $495 before its other credits.

Which card is kinder to guests in lounges? The Sapphire Reserve. Two guests enter free. The Venture X charges $35 to $45 per adult guest.

Do both cards use Priority Pass? Yes, both include a Priority Pass membership with 1,300+ lounges. Priority Pass restaurants are not included on the Reserve.
