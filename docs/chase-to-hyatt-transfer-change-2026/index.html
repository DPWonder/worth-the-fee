Did Chase change the Hyatt transfer rate, and which Sapphire card still transfers 1:1?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Yes. From a Sapphire Preferred, 4 Chase points now become 3 World of Hyatt points, where it used to be 1 for 1. The Sapphire Reserve still transfers to Hyatt at 1:1.

What changed and when

Date What happened
June 15, 2026 New Sapphire Preferred cardholders approved from this date got the 4:3 Hyatt rate from the start
September 30, 2026 Last day of 1:1 for existing Sapphire Preferred cardholders
October 1, 2026 Existing Sapphire Preferred cardholders moved to 4:3

The Ink Business Preferred and the older Ink Plus moved to 4:3 as well.

Only Hyatt changed. Reports say the other airline and hotel partners still transfer 1:1 from the Preferred, including United and Southwest.

Which card gets which rate

Card Chase points to Hyatt points
Sapphire Preferred 4 to 3
Ink Business Preferred 4 to 3
Sapphire Reserve 1 to 1
Sapphire Reserve for Business 1 to 1
J.P. Morgan Reserve 1 to 1

Chase's public Sapphire Preferred page lists World of Hyatt as a partner but does not show the ratio. The figures here come from The Points Guy, AwardWallet and NerdWallet, which agree with each other.

What it costs in points

At 4:3, the same Hyatt night takes one third more Chase points.

The Preferred figures are the Hyatt amount multiplied by four thirds. The minimum transfer size and step were not confirmed in the sources read, so the amount you must send may round up further.

Another way to look at it: a Chase point moved to Hyatt from a Preferred is now worth 75% of what it was. If you valued a Hyatt point at 2 cents, a Preferred point sent there is worth 1.5 cents. That is the same as the usual top Points Boost value on the Preferred, so the gap between transferring and booking through Chase Travel is smaller than it was.

If you hold both cards

Chase lets you move points between your own cards. If you have a Sapphire Reserve as well as a Preferred, you can move the Preferred's points into the Reserve account and transfer to Hyatt from there at 1:1. Both AwardWallet and The Points Guy describe this.

That only helps if the Reserve makes sense for you on its own. The Reserve costs $795 a year against $95 for the Preferred. The $700 gap is a lot to pay for a transfer rate. As a rough test, at 2 cents per Hyatt point you would need to move more than 140,000 points a year to Hyatt before the better rate alone covered $700. Most people will decide on the Reserve's credits and not on this.

Things to know before any Hyatt transfer

  • Transfers cannot be reversed. Once points are in Hyatt, they stay there.
  • Transfers are instant in most cases, so there is rarely a reason to move points before you have found a room.
  • Hyatt points expire after 24 months with no qualifying activity.
  • Hyatt changed its award chart in May 2026. Each category now has five price levels, so the same hotel can cost very different amounts on different nights. Check the price for your dates first.

Who should skip it

  • Sapphire Preferred holders who rarely stay at Hyatt. Other partners still transfer 1:1, and Chase Travel with a Boost may come close.
  • Anyone thinking of the Reserve only for the Hyatt rate, unless they move a large number of points each year.
  • Anyone with no specific booking in mind. Moving points on a guess locks them into one program.

Quick answers

Is the Sapphire Preferred to Hyatt rate still 1:1? No. It has been 4:3 since October 1, 2026, and since June 15, 2026 for cards opened from that date.

Did other Chase partners change too? Reports say no. Only Hyatt moved, and only from the Preferred and Ink cards listed above.

Can I get 1:1 by moving points to a Reserve first? Yes, if the Reserve account is yours. Points moved there transfer to Hyatt at 1:1.
