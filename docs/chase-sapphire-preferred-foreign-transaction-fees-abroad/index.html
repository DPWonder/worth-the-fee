Is the Chase Sapphire Preferred a good card to use abroad?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

For purchases, yes. The Sapphire Preferred charges no foreign transaction fee, runs on the Visa network, and carries trip and rental car protections that apply outside the United States. It is a poor way to get cash, it has no lounge access, and it does not pay medical bills abroad.

The fee, in numbers

Many US cards add about 3% to each purchase made in a foreign currency. The Sapphire Preferred adds nothing, according to Chase's card page.

Spending abroad Card with a 3% foreign fee Sapphire Preferred
$1,000 $30 in fees $0
$3,000 $90 in fees $0
$5,000 $150 in fees $0

The card's annual fee is $95. A card with no annual fee and no foreign fee would save the same amounts, so the foreign fee alone does not justify the $95.

Acceptance

The card is a Visa Signature card, per FinanceBuzz, and Visa is widely accepted worldwide. FinanceBuzz also reports that you do not need to tell Chase before you travel.

Whether the card works with a PIN at unattended machines, such as some train ticket kiosks and fuel pumps in Europe, is not confirmed. Search summaries describe it as a chip-and-signature card. Carry a second way to pay.

Three ways it can still cost you

Paying in dollars at the terminal. Some foreign terminals offer to charge you in US dollars. That is dynamic currency conversion: the merchant sets the exchange rate, and Wise notes the rate can be inflated. Choosing the local currency leaves the conversion to Visa.

Cash from an ATM. A cash withdrawal on the card is a cash advance. Wise reports the fee as $10 or 5% of each transaction, whichever is greater, plus any fee from the ATM owner. A $400 withdrawal costs $20 before interest. A debit card is the usual tool for cash.

Cash-like purchases. Buying foreign currency or traveler's checks does not count as a purchase and earns no points.

Chase's summary puts no US-only limit on the dining or travel categories. No source read for this page states in so many words that foreign restaurants earn 3 points, and the rate depends on how the merchant is coded. Gas, online grocery and streaming categories have their own exclusions.

Protections that travel with you

These apply when the trip or rental is paid with the card. Limits and exclusions are on the travel insurance page.

  • Trip cancellation and interruption, up to $10,000 per traveler
  • Trip delay, up to $500 per traveler after 12 hours or an overnight stay
  • Baggage delay, up to $100 a day for 5 days
  • Lost luggage, up to $3,000 per traveler, with reported sub-limits of $500 for jewelry and watches and $500 for cameras and electronics
  • Primary rental car cover for damage to the car, in the United States and some countries abroad

The card also has a travel and emergency assistance line. FinanceBuzz describes it as a referral service: it helps you find a doctor, a lawyer or a translator, and you pay for whatever you use.

What it lacks abroad

  • Medical cover. No emergency medical expense benefit is listed for this card.
  • Liability cover in a rental car. The rental benefit covers the car, not other people or property.
  • Airport lounges. None are included. The Sapphire Reserve has them, at a much higher fee.

The card also has a credit of up to $120 every four years for a Global Entry, TSA PreCheck or NEXUS application fee, which helps on the way home.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone who mainly needs cash abroad. Use a debit card with low ATM fees.
  • Anyone who wants medical cover from a card. Buy a travel medical policy.
  • Anyone who travels abroad rarely and would not use the card's other features. A no-annual-fee card without a foreign fee saves the same 3%.
  • Anyone who wants lounge access.

Quick answers

Does the Sapphire Preferred charge a foreign transaction fee? No. Chase's card page lists no foreign transaction fee on purchases made outside the United States.

Should I pay in dollars or the local currency? The local currency. When a terminal converts to dollars for you, the merchant picks the rate, and it can be worse than Visa's.

Can I use it at an ATM abroad? Yes, but it is a cash advance. The reported fee is $10 or 5%, whichever is greater, plus ATM owner fees.
