Card comparisons: every short answer on one page

Each answer links to its full page, with sources and the date it was checked. This is information, not financial advice.

Amex Gold or Chase Sapphire Preferred for someone who spends most on food?

The Amex Gold fits a food spender who buys a lot at US supermarkets, because it pays 4 points per dollar there and the Sapphire Preferred pays 1. For someone whose food spending is mostly restaurants, the gap is 1 point per dollar, and the $95 Preferred usually costs less overall.

Amex Business Platinum vs personal Platinum: how do the fee and credits differ?

Both cards cost $895 a year and share the same lounge access, hotel status and $600 hotel credit. The difference is in the other credits: the personal Platinum's are for dining, streaming, Uber and retail, and the Business Platinum's are for business costs such as Dell, Indeed, Adobe and a phone bill.

Amex Platinum or Capital One Venture X: which fee is easier to earn back?

The Venture X fee is easier to earn back. One $300 booking through Capital One Travel plus the 10,000 anniversary miles covers its $395 fee, while the $895 Platinum fee takes several separate credits, most of them tied to named brands.

Amex Platinum or Chase Sapphire Reserve: which fee is easier to earn back?

For most travelers the Sapphire Reserve fee is easier to earn back: its $300 travel credit applies to any travel purchase without enrollment, which leaves $495 of the $795 fee to cover. The $895 Platinum fee comes back only through several credits tied to named brands, so it fits people who already spend with those brands or who use Centurion and Delta lounges.

Amex Platinum or Delta SkyMiles Reserve for a Delta flyer who wants lounge access?

For someone who flies only Delta, the Delta SkyMiles Reserve is the closer fit: it costs $650, gives 15 Sky Club visits a year against the Platinum's 10, and adds free checked bags and a companion certificate. The $895 Platinum fits a Delta flyer who also flies other airlines, since its Centurion and Priority Pass access does not depend on a Delta ticket.

Amex Platinum or Hilton Honors Aspire for someone who stays at Hilton?

For someone whose hotel nights are mostly at Hilton, the Hilton Honors Aspire gives more for less: a $550 fee, top-tier Diamond status, 14 points per dollar at Hilton and a free night each year. The $895 Platinum Card gives Hilton Gold status and only 1 point per dollar on most Hilton bills; its strengths are airport lounges and points that move to airlines.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or Amex Green: which mid-fee travel card fits?

For a new applicant the choice is made already: Amex stopped taking applications for the Green Card in July 2026, and the $95 Sapphire Preferred is open. The Green Card, now named the Classic Green Card, still exists at $150 for current holders and as a downgrade from an Amex Gold or Platinum card, and it earns more on travel booked direct.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or a Bilt card for a renter?

A renter who would put most everyday spending on the card earns more with a Bilt card, because rent itself earns up to 1.25 points per dollar. A renter who spends little on the card, or who wants strong trip and rental car cover, is closer to the Sapphire Preferred, since Bilt's rent points shrink to 250 a month when other spending is low.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or Capital One Venture: which fits a few trips a year?

Both cards cost $95 a year, so the choice comes down to where the money goes. The Venture earns more for someone whose spending is mostly general purchases, and the Sapphire Preferred earns more for someone whose dining and similar spending is larger than everything else, and it has stronger trip protection.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or Citi Strata Premier: which $95-class card fits?

Both cards cost $95 a year. The Citi Strata Premier earns more for someone with large supermarket and airfare spending, and the Sapphire Preferred fits someone who wants stronger trip protection or points that move to United, Southwest and Hyatt.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or Freedom Unlimited: is the annual fee worth paying over the no-fee card?

The $95 Sapphire Preferred is worth paying for if you will use its $100 hotel credit, spend abroad, or move points to airline and hotel programs. If you want cash back and travel little, the no-fee Freedom Unlimited returns more, because it pays 1.5% on ordinary purchases where the Preferred pays 1 point.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or a Southwest Rapid Rewards card for a Southwest flyer?

A Southwest flyer who checks bags gets more from a Southwest Rapid Rewards card, because every Southwest card waives the first bag fee for up to nine people on a reservation. A Southwest flyer who travels with a carry-on, or who also flies other airlines, gets more from the $95 Sapphire Preferred, whose points move to Southwest at 1:1.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or United Explorer for someone who mostly flies United?

A United flyer who checks a bag on two round trips a year saves more in bag fees than the United Explorer's $150 fee. A United flyer who travels with a carry-on gets little from the Explorer and more from the $95 Sapphire Preferred, whose points move to United at 1:1 and to 15 other programs.

Chase Sapphire Preferred or Wells Fargo Autograph Journey: which $95 card fits?

Both cards cost $95 a year, and the Wells Fargo Autograph Journey earns more points on flights and hotels booked directly, at 4x and 5x against the Sapphire Preferred's 2x. The Preferred fits a reader who wants points that move to United, Southwest and Hyatt, or primary rental car cover in the United States.

Chase Sapphire Reserve or Capital One Venture X: which premium card costs less to keep?

The Venture X costs less to keep. After its $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles its $395 fee nets to about zero, while the Sapphire Reserve's $795 fee nets to $495 after its $300 travel credit, and the rest has to come from narrower credits.

Chase Sapphire Reserve or Citi Strata Elite: which premium card costs less to keep?

On the fee alone the Citi Strata Elite costs less, at $595 a year against $795, but the Sapphire Reserve's $300 credit works on any travel and brings it to $495. The Strata Elite gets to $295 only with a two-night hotel stay booked through Citi Travel, so it is the cheaper card to keep for readers who would make that booking anyway.
