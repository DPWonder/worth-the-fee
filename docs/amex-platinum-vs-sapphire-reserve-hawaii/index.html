Amex Platinum or Chase Sapphire Reserve for a once-a-year Hawaii trip?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

For one Hawaii trip a year, the Chase Sapphire Reserve is the closer fit of the two. It costs $100 less, its $300 travel credit works on any travel, and its points move to Hyatt 1:1. Neither card is an easy case for a single trip: both fees are high and lounge access in Hawaii is thin.

How the credits fit one trip

The Amex hotel credit is split by half-year. One trip can use only one $300 half unless you book a second stay in the other half. The Hotel Collection and Airbnb Luxe need two nights.

The Reserve's hotel credit is $250 per stay. One Hawaii trip with two separate hotel stays of two nights or more, both at The Edit hotels, could use all $500. One stay uses $250.

Most other credits on both cards are monthly or quarterly and do nothing for the trip itself. Count them only if you already pay for those things. The Reserve's $300 travel credit is the one credit on either card that a once-a-year traveler will use without trying.

Lounges at Honolulu and Maui

Airport Amex Platinum Sapphire Reserve
Honolulu (HNL) Amex lists Delta Sky Club (Terminal 2), IASS Hawaii Lounge (Terminal 2) and Hawaiian Airlines Premier Club (Terminal 1) Chase lists one: IASS Hawaii Lounge (Terminal 2)
Maui (OGG) Two Hawaiian Airlines Premier Clubs, through Priority Pass The same two Hawaiian Airlines Premier Clubs, through Priority Pass
  • There is no Amex Centurion Lounge at either airport in Amex's lounge finder.
  • The Delta Sky Club is only for Platinum holders flying Delta that day, and the card allows 10 visits a year.
  • The IASS lounge has no food, according to Chase. Its Priority Pass hours are reported as 7:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. and 2 p.m. to 6 p.m., which misses evening flights home.
  • Sources disagree on whether the Hawaiian Premier Club at Honolulu takes Priority Pass. Amex lists it; Chase's Honolulu guide does not. Check the Priority Pass app before you rely on it.
  • At Maui the lounges offer snacks and drinks, entry starts 2 hours before departure, and they can turn people away when full.

The Platinum is ahead at Honolulu only if you fly Delta. Otherwise the cards are close to equal in Hawaii.

Using points for Hawaii hotels

Here the cards differ most. Chase points from a Sapphire Reserve move to Hyatt at 1:1. Amex points do not move to Hyatt at all. The Amex hotel partners are Hilton at 1:2, Marriott at 1:1 and Choice at 1:1.

Hyatt prices rooms by category. Its top category runs from 35,000 to 75,000 points a night. Guides place several Hawaii resorts there, but sources conflict on some hotels after Hyatt's May 2026 chart change, so look up the exact hotel and dates on Hyatt's site before moving points. At those prices, 100,000 points covers one or two nights, not a week.

The Platinum does give Hilton Gold and Marriott Gold status, which helps if you pay cash at those chains.

Who should get neither

  • Anyone who takes one trip a year and would not use the monthly credits. A card with a fee under $100 costs far less to hold.
  • Anyone who carries a balance.
  • For the Reserve: anyone who has opened five or more personal cards in 24 months, since Chase generally declines them.

Quick answers

Which card has the lower fee? The Sapphire Reserve at $795. The Amex Platinum is $895.

Is there a Centurion Lounge in Hawaii? Not at Honolulu or Maui, according to Amex's own lounge finder.

Can I move Amex points to Hyatt? No. Hyatt is a Chase partner. From the Sapphire Reserve the rate is 1:1.
