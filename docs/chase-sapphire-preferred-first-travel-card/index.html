Is the Chase Sapphire Preferred a sensible first travel card, and what credit do you need?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

It can be, for someone who already has good credit, at least a year or two of credit history, and one or more trips a year. Chase publishes no minimum score; guides suggest 670 to 700 or higher. It is a poor first credit card of any kind, because approval is harder with a thin credit file.

What credit you need

Chase does not publish a cutoff. Guides differ:

Source Suggested score Other advice
Upgraded Points (July 2025) At least 670 About 2 years of good history; income of $30,000 or more
NerdWallet (search summary) At least 690 Not read in full
The Points Guy (July 2026) At least 700 Average account age over one year; balances under about 20% of limits

Upgraded Points notes people have been approved with scores in the 600s and denied with scores in the 800s. Chase also looks at income, debt and account age.

Upgraded Points reports a minimum credit limit of $5,000 for new cardholders, which is why very low incomes are a hurdle.

The other approval rule

Chase generally declines applicants who have opened five or more personal credit cards, from any bank, in the past 24 months. A first-time travel card applicant is rarely near that number, but authorized-user cards can count. See the 5/24 rule.

If you are declined, Chase must tell you why. The Points Guy notes you can call Chase's reconsideration line and ask for a second look; it does not always work.

Welcome offers vary by applicant and change over time. The public offer checked on this date required $5,000 of purchases in the first 3 months. Only count an offer if that spending is already in your budget.

The sums for a beginner

The fee is $95 a year. Ways it can be covered:

  • One hotel stay through Chase Travel. The credit of up to $100 offsets the fee, if the Chase Travel price is as low as booking direct.
  • Points on ordinary spending. $400 a month on dining earns 14,400 points a year, worth $144 as cash at 1 cent each. A 2% cash back card with no fee would pay $96 on the same spending, so the card's edge on that spending is $48.
  • Protections. Primary rental car cover and trip insurance have value only if you rent cars or prepay trips.

If you take no trips in a year and book no hotel through Chase Travel, the points on everyday spending have to carry the whole fee. A worked year is on the first-year plan page.

Why it is a common first travel card

  • The fee is low for a card with transferable points and primary rental car cover.
  • Points can be used simply, as cash or through Chase Travel, before you learn transfers.
  • The Points Guy says approval is generally easier than for the Sapphire Reserve.

Limits a beginner should know

  • No airport lounge access.
  • No medical cover abroad.
  • Transfers to Hyatt are now 4 Chase points for 3 Hyatt points.
  • Chase may not give the new cardmember bonus to someone who has held the card before. See bonus eligibility.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone with no credit history, or less than a year of it. The Points Guy suggests building credit with a starter card first.
  • Anyone with a score well under 670.
  • Anyone who carries a balance month to month.
  • Anyone who does not travel at least once a year. A no-fee cash back card is simpler.
  • Anyone who has opened five or more cards in the past 24 months.

Quick answers

What credit score do I need for the Sapphire Preferred? Chase publishes no minimum. Guides suggest at least 670 to 700, and approval also depends on income and history.

Can the Sapphire Preferred be my first credit card ever? It is unlikely to be approved with no credit history. Guides suggest a year or two of on-time payments on another card first.

Is the $95 fee waived in the first year? No. The fee is charged in the first year.
