# Is Rakuten legit, and how does it make money if it pays you?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/is-rakuten-legit-how-it-makes-money/
Facts checked: 2026-11-27
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Yes, Rakuten is legit: it is a real company, owned by Rakuten Group of Tokyo, whose members have earned cash back since 1999. Stores pay it a commission for each shopper it sends, and it passes part of that commission to you; in return it collects data about what you browse and buy.

This page is information, not financial advice. Rakuten's own pages were re-read on November 27, 2026. For what it costs to use, see [is Rakuten free](/is-rakuten-free/); for the joining step, [how to sign up for Rakuten](/how-to-sign-up-for-rakuten/).

## How the money flows

Rakuten's help center puts it in one line: "Stores pay Rakuten a commission for referring shoppers to them." Rakuten keeps a portion and gives the rest to the member as cash back. What portion it keeps is not confirmed; Rakuten does not publish it.

This is affiliate marketing. The store pays only when a sale happens, so it is willing to share part of the sale price. The cash back does not come from a membership fee. Rakuten says joining is free. Confirmed cash back is paid four times a year; see [when Rakuten pays](/rakuten-when-do-you-get-paid/) and [how Rakuten works, step by step](/how-does-rakuten-work-step-by-step/).

Rakuten's terms list other income too: marketing funds, cost-per-click fees and commissions on gift card redemptions. They say this compensation may play a part in which stores and products appear on the site, where they are placed and how they are promoted. So the order of stores on a page is not a neutral ranking. The terms also say Rakuten funds some offers from its own money. New-member bonuses are one kind of offer; the conditions are on [how the sign-up and referral bonuses work](/rakuten-50-bonus-how-it-works/).

Sources differ slightly on the founding year. One encyclopedia entry says Ebates was founded in 1998 and its site launched in May 1999; Rakuten dates its cash back from 1999. Rakuten's Black Friday page gives 1997, which is the parent company's founding year.

## What data it collects

Rakuten's privacy policy carries the date October 23, 2026. It lists these kinds of data:

- Name, postal and email address, and phone number.
- IP address, device and session IDs.
- Payment account name and number, card expiration date and billing address.
- Searches, clicks, cart items, store visits, purchases and reward balances.
- URLs you visit, click stream and timestamps, including through the browser extension and the app.
- Precise location, only if you turn it on for the app on your device.

The policy says the browser extension and the app collect data about your online activity on that device even when you are not interacting with them. To withdraw consent for that, you uninstall them. You can still earn by starting at the website each time. More on this is in [is the Rakuten browser extension safe](/rakuten-browser-extension-safe-privacy/).

## Who it shares data with

The policy names affiliate networks (click data and a tracking ID, so the store can credit your purchase), stores, referral and loyalty partners, financial partners, digital advertising providers and other Rakuten Group companies. It says some of these disclosures may be considered selling or sharing under US state privacy laws. Rakuten's home page says it does not sell personal data to data brokers, and that it may share limited information with advertising partners.

You have options. Members can opt out in the Privacy Preference Center, and the policy says Rakuten honors Global Privacy Control signals from a browser. You can also ask to see, correct or delete your data.

The policy also says offers and cash back rates may be personalized based on your account history. Two members may see different rates at the same store.

## Limits written into the terms

- Cash back is not final until the store confirms it. Rakuten may deduct it for returns and cancellations.
- If a store does not pay Rakuten for a transaction, Rakuten may cancel the cash back tied to it.
- After 12 months with no shopping trip, Rakuten may take a $5 monthly maintenance fee from your balance. The fee stops when the balance reaches zero.
- You must report missing cash back within 90 days of the purchase. See [what to do when cash back did not track](/rakuten-missing-cash-back-what-to-do/).

## Who should skip it

- You do not want a company recording which sites you visit. The extension and app do that by design.
- You rarely shop online. With one or two small orders a year, the balance may sit unused and the inactivity fee can reduce it.
- You always check out with coupon codes from other sites. Codes not listed on Rakuten can void the cash back.

## Quick answers

**Does Rakuten cost anything to join?**
No. Rakuten says sign-up is free. Its income is the commission stores pay.

**Who owns Rakuten in the US?**
Rakuten Group, Inc. of Tokyo. It bought Ebates in 2014 and renamed it in 2019.

**Can you stop Rakuten collecting browsing data?**
Uninstall the extension and app, and use the opt-out in the Privacy Preference Center.
