Is Rakuten legit, and how does it make money if it pays you?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

The short answer

Yes, Rakuten is a real company that has paid cash back since 1999. Stores pay it a commission for each shopper it sends, and it passes part of that commission to you; in return it collects data about what you browse and buy.

How the money flows

Rakuten's help center puts it in one line: "Stores pay Rakuten a commission for referring shoppers to them." Rakuten keeps a portion and gives the rest to the member as cash back.

This is affiliate marketing. The store pays only when a sale happens, so it is willing to share part of the sale price. The cash back does not come from a membership fee. Rakuten says joining is free. Confirmed cash back is paid four times a year; see when Rakuten pays.

Rakuten's terms add that it generally receives compensation from brands and other partners, and that this may play a part in which stores and products appear on the site. So the order of stores on a page is not a neutral ranking.

What data it collects

Rakuten's privacy policy was updated on October 23, 2026. It lists these kinds of data:

  • Name, postal and email address, and phone number.
  • IP address, device and session IDs.
  • Payment account name and number, card expiration date and billing address.
  • Searches, clicks, cart items, store visits, purchases and reward balances.
  • URLs you visit, click stream and timestamps, including through the browser extension and the app.
  • Precise location, only if you turn it on for the app on your device.

The policy says the browser extension and the app collect data about your online activity on that device even when you are not interacting with them. To withdraw consent for that, you uninstall them. You can still earn by starting at the website each time.

Who it shares data with

The policy names affiliate networks (click data and a tracking ID, so the store can credit your purchase), stores, referral and loyalty partners, financial partners, digital advertising providers and other Rakuten Group companies. It says some of these disclosures may be considered selling or sharing under US state privacy laws.

You have options. Members can opt out in the Privacy Preference Center, and the policy says Rakuten honors Global Privacy Control signals from a browser. You can also ask to see, correct or delete your data.

The policy also says offers and cash back rates may be personalized based on your account history. Two members may see different rates at the same store.

Limits written into the terms

  • Cash back is not final until the store confirms it. Rakuten may deduct it for returns and cancellations.
  • If a store does not pay Rakuten for a transaction, Rakuten may cancel the cash back tied to it.
  • After 12 months with no shopping trip, Rakuten may take a $5 monthly maintenance fee from your balance. The fee cannot push the balance below zero.
  • You must report missing cash back within 90 days of the purchase. See what to do when cash back did not track.

Who should skip it

  • You do not want a company recording which sites you visit. The extension and app do that by design.
  • You rarely shop online. With one or two small orders a year, the balance may sit unused and the inactivity fee can reduce it.
  • You always check out with coupon codes from other sites. Codes not listed on Rakuten can void the cash back.

Quick answers

Does Rakuten cost anything to join? No. Rakuten says sign-up is free. Its income is the commission stores pay.

Who owns Rakuten in the US? Rakuten Group, Inc. of Tokyo. It bought Ebates in 2014 and renamed it in 2019.

Can you stop Rakuten collecting browsing data? Uninstall the extension and app, and use the opt-out in the Privacy Preference Center.
