Which Chase transfer partners are worth using, and which rarely are?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Of Chase's 16 transfer partners, Hyatt, Virgin Atlantic, Air Canada Aeroplan, Flying Blue and the Avios programs are the ones published valuations put clearly above the 1 cent a point is worth in Chase Travel. Marriott, IHG and Wyndham are valued below 1 cent, so a transfer to them usually loses value unless a bonus is running.

The test: does it beat 1 cent?

A Chase point is worth 1 cent toward any booking in Chase Travel, and more on some bookings; see how Points Boost works. A transfer is worth doing only when the award you book returns more than that. Transfers cannot be reversed.

Transfers are 1 to 1 in blocks of 1,000 points. The one exception is Hyatt from a Sapphire Preferred or Ink Business Preferred, which is 4 to 3; see the Hyatt change. You need a Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve, Ink Business Preferred or Sapphire Reserve for Business to transfer at all.

Cathay Pacific Asia Miles and Qantas Frequent Flyer were added on November 18, 2026, which brought the count to 16.

Partners that tend to be worth it

  • Hyatt. It has a fixed award chart, and AwardWallet puts standard rooms from 3,500 points a night at the lowest category. From a Sapphire Preferred the 4 to 3 ratio cuts the return by a quarter.
  • Virgin Atlantic. The Points Guy reports economy between New York and London from 6,000 points plus about $165 in taxes and fees, and business class from 29,000 points plus about $700 in surcharges one way. The cash part is large; count it.
  • Flying Blue. Business class between the US and Europe starts at 60,000 miles one way, with taxes and fees near $600 in Flying Blue's own example.
  • Iberia. Off-peak business class from New York or Boston to Madrid is 40,500 Avios one way, per The Points Guy and AwardWallet.
  • United and Southwest. Lower values per point, but simple to use on US flights. AwardWallet lists United domestic awards from 5,000 miles one way.

Partners that rarely are

  • Marriott, IHG and Wyndham. At 0.55 to 0.75 cents, the same points buy more as a plain Chase Travel booking. AwardWallet says of IHG that transfers are generally not a good deal.
  • Singapore KrisFlyer adds fuel surcharges on some flights, and transfers take a day or two, so a seat can go while you wait.

Where sources disagree

  • Wyndham. The Points Guy values a point at 0.55 cents. AwardWallet's users report 1.33 cents on average, and more at some all-inclusive resorts.
  • Marriott. The Points Guy says 0.75 cents; AwardWallet's users average 0.93 cents.
  • Transfer bonuses. AwardWallet lists bonuses now: 30% to the Avios programs through December 1, 40% to Virgin Atlantic through December 30, 80% to IHG and 30% to Wyndham through December 3, 2026. These were not confirmed on a Chase page. With an 80% bonus, IHG comes to about 1.08 cents per Chase point at The Points Guy's value. Check the transfer screen for the ratio that applies to you.

Who should skip transfers

  • People who will not search for award seats before moving points.
  • People who mostly book hotels other than Hyatt.
  • Sapphire Preferred holders eyeing Hyatt who have not redone the sums at 4 to 3.

Quick answers

How many transfer partners does Chase have? Sixteen as of November 18, 2026: twelve airline programs and four hotel programs, per AwardWallet.

Is every Chase transfer 1 to 1? Yes, except Hyatt from a Sapphire Preferred or Ink Business Preferred, which is 4 to 3.

Can a transfer be undone? No. Sources agree that transfers are final, so find the award seat or room first.
