Does pairing a Sapphire Preferred with Freedom cards make sense, and how do you combine the points?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Pairing a Sapphire Preferred with a no-fee Freedom card makes sense for people who transfer points to airlines or hotels, because Freedom points can be moved to the Preferred for free and sent to partners from there. Since October 2025 the move no longer raises what the points are worth on ordinary Chase Travel bookings, so for someone who takes points as cash the pairing adds little.

How to combine points

Chase's own guide says there is no minimum and no fee, and no points are lost.

  1. Sign in to the Chase account and open the rewards section for the card that holds the points.
  2. Open Rewards details, then Combine points.
  3. Pick the card to move from and the card to move to.
  4. Enter the amount, review and submit. Chase may ask for a security code.

Between your own cards the move is instant, per The Points Guy. Only the primary cardholder can do it.

Another person. Chase lets you share with one person at the same address: "you can only designate one person to share points with." The first link is set up by phone. Chase may suspend or close accounts for misuse, such as selling points.

Undoing it. Chase's guide says a transfer cannot be undone. AwardWallet says points can be moved straight back between your own cards. Treat a move to another person as final.

If the option is missing. One site reports that some people lost the online option in late 2025 and had a phone agent move the points.

What changed in 2025

Before, points held on a Sapphire Preferred were worth a fixed 1.25 cents each toward any Chase Travel booking. Moving Freedom points over raised their value by a quarter at once.

That ended with Points Boost. Frequent Miler reports that points earned on, or combined to, a Sapphire card from October 26, 2025 follow the new rules: 1 cent as the base, more only on bookings marked with a Boost. On the Preferred a Boost goes up to 1.5 cents, or 1.75 on some premium-cabin fares; sources differ on that top figure. Details are on the Points Boost page. Chase's own wording for that rule was not found, so the date rests on third-party reports.

So the reason to combine now is transfers to partners, plus the occasional Boost. One more change: from the Preferred, Hyatt is 4 Chase points to 3, not 1:1. See the Hyatt transfer change.

The sum

A Freedom Flex user who fills every quarter earns 5 points on $6,000 a year in place of 1: 24,000 extra points. That takes activating each quarter and spending in the listed categories.

Costs to set against it:

  • A second card is a new account with a hard credit check, and it counts toward 5/24.
  • The Freedom Unlimited charges a 3% foreign transaction fee. Keep it at home.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone who redeems points as cash. Points are worth 1 cent on either card, so the Preferred's $95 fee buys nothing in the pairing; a Freedom card alone does the job.
  • Anyone who does not want to track categories across several cards.
  • Anyone saving 5/24 slots for other applications.
  • Anyone whose spending is mostly dining, gas and travel. The Preferred already earns 3x or 2x there.

Quick answers

Can Freedom points be transferred to airlines and hotels? Not directly. Move them to a Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve or Ink Business Preferred first, then transfer.

Is there a fee or a minimum to combine Chase points? No. Chase says there is no fee and no minimum amount.

Does moving points to the Preferred still make them worth 1.25 cents? No. Since October 26, 2025, newly earned or combined points get 1 cent, or more only on Points Boost bookings.
