# What does Priority Pass include with the Amex Platinum versus the Sapphire Reserve?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/priority-pass-by-card-what-you-get/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Both cards come with a Priority Pass Select membership: unlimited lounge visits, two guests free, and no Priority Pass restaurant credits. The differences are small: Chase enrolls you automatically and puts a digital card in its app, and its version still covers some non-lounge stops such as Minute Suites, while Amex makes you enroll and covers lounges only.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## Side by side

| | Amex Platinum ($895 a year) | Chase Sapphire Reserve ($795 a year) |
|---|---|---|
| Membership | Priority Pass Select | Priority Pass Select |
| Enrollment | Required; a card is mailed | Automatic since October 25, 2025; digital card in the Chase app |
| Lounge visits | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Free guests | Two per visit, where the lounge takes guests | Two per visit |
| Extra guests | Pay the Priority Pass guest fee; amount not confirmed | $27 each |
| Priority Pass restaurants | Not included | Not included since July 1, 2024 |
| Minute Suites, Be Relax spas, Gameway | Not included | Included (first hour at Minute Suites and Gameway) |
| Authorized users | Each $195 additional Platinum cardholder can enroll in their own membership | Each $195 authorized user gets a membership with two guests |
| Chase Sapphire Lounges | Not through Priority Pass | Yes, with the Reserve card itself |

Sources: The Points Guy (August 2026), Upgraded Points, AwardWallet (March 2026) and Chase's lounge page.

## Guests

Both memberships let you bring two guests at no charge. On the Reserve, a third guest costs $27. On the Platinum, extra guests pay the fee Priority Pass sets; none of the pages read gave the amount.

A guest allowance is not a guarantee. Chase's terms say entry can be refused when a lounge is full, and The Points Guy notes that some lounges limit the hours they take Priority Pass members.

An authorized user is often the cheaper route for a regular travel partner: on either card, $195 a year gives that person their own membership and their own two guests. For the rest of what that fee buys on the Platinum, see [the $195 authorized user card](/amex-platinum-authorized-user-worth-195/).

## Restaurants and other stops

Priority Pass sells some memberships that include a credit at airport restaurants. Neither card's version has it.

- **Amex Platinum:** restaurants are not included, per The Points Guy and Upgraded Points. Upgraded Points also lists Minute Suites, Be Relax and Gameway as not included.
- **Sapphire Reserve:** restaurants have been out since July 1, 2024. AwardWallet lists what still works: Be Relax and XWELL spa treatments, the first hour in a Minute Suite, an hour in a Gameway lounge with a snack and a drink, and the golf simulator at Denver.

The Priority Pass app shows which stops your own membership covers at each airport.

## How many lounges

Sources give different counts. Upgraded Points says more than 1,400 for the Platinum and more than 1,300 for the Reserve; Chase says 1,300+; The Points Guy says more than 1,900 for the Platinum; AwardWallet says Priority Pass as a whole has more than 1,800.

No source read shows a lounge that one card's Priority Pass opens and the other's does not, apart from the non-lounge stops above. Treat the two lounge lists as the same until the app says otherwise.

## What Priority Pass is not

Priority Pass is one part of each card's lounge access, and the larger differences lie outside it.

- **Amex Platinum** also has Centurion Lounges, 10 Delta Sky Club visits a year when flying Delta, Escape and Plaza Premium lounges. See [Centurion guest rules](/amex-platinum-centurion-lounge-guest-rules/).
- **Sapphire Reserve** also has Chase Sapphire Lounges and Air Canada Maple Leaf Lounges on Star Alliance flights. See [Sapphire Reserve lounge access](/chase-sapphire-reserve-lounge-access/).

Chase Sapphire Lounges left the Priority Pass network on August 15, 2026. A Priority Pass card from the Amex Platinum no longer opens them.

Priority Pass coverage is thin at some airports. Honolulu is an example; see [Hawaii airport lounges by card](/hawaii-airport-lounges-by-card/).

## Where sources disagree

- **Sapphire Lounges with an Amex Priority Pass.** Upgraded Points' comparison page still describes one free Sapphire Lounge visit a year for Amex Priority Pass members, then $75. Upgraded Points' own news item and The Points Guy report that the lounges left Priority Pass on August 15, 2026. The later reports are used here.
- **Lounge counts,** as given above.

## Who should skip paying for either card for Priority Pass

- People who fly a few times a year from an airport with no Priority Pass lounge in their terminal. Check the Priority Pass site for your home airport first.
- People who wanted the restaurant credit. Neither card has it.
- Families of five or more. Two free guests cover three people.
- People who would use only Priority Pass and none of the card's other lounges or credits. At $795 or $895 a year, the fee has to be earned back elsewhere; see [what the Platinum credits are worth](/amex-platinum-credits-real-value/) and [what the Reserve credits are worth](/chase-sapphire-reserve-credits-real-value/).

## Quick answers

**Can I use Priority Pass restaurants with either card?**
No. The Amex Platinum version excludes them, and Chase removed them from the Sapphire Reserve on July 1, 2024.

**How many guests can I bring?**
Two free on either card. The Sapphire Reserve charges $27 for each extra guest.

**Do I have to enroll?**
With the Amex Platinum, yes. With the Sapphire Reserve, enrollment has been automatic since October 25, 2025.
