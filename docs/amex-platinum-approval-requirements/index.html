What credit score and income do you need for the Amex Platinum?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

The short answer

American Express publishes no minimum credit score and no minimum income for the Platinum Card. Guides describe the usual applicant as having good to excellent credit, and Amex's "Apply With Confidence" process can tell a new applicant whether they are approved before any hard inquiry is placed on their credit report.

What Amex says, and what guides say

Upgraded Points puts it plainly: "American Express does not disclose a minimum credit score requirement." The numbers below are estimates from review sites, not rules.

Source Credit score Income
American Express No minimum published No minimum published
Upgraded Points (July 2026) FICO 670 or higher can improve the chances $50,000 or more may improve the odds
CNBC Select (November 2026) Good to excellent; no number given No number; higher income helps
Other sites seen in search results 690 and 720 Not given

The sources disagree on a number, and none of them is the issuer. CNBC Select notes that good credit does not guarantee approval.

What else Amex looks at

A score is one input. Guides and Amex's own card agreement name others:

  • Income and the resources Amex knows about.
  • Your history with Amex, if you have other Amex cards.
  • How many cards you have open and how recently you opened them.
  • Your payment history and balances on other accounts.

The Platinum Card has no preset spending limit, so there is no fixed credit line to qualify for. The annual fee is $895.

Amex can check any of the three credit bureaus. Upgraded Points says applicants most often report Experian.

How the pre-approval notice works

Amex calls it Apply With Confidence. The steps, as described by Bankrate and The Points Guy:

  1. You fill in the normal application: name, address, income, Social Security number.
  2. A notice on the form says you can learn whether you are approved with no effect on your credit score.
  3. You submit. Amex runs a soft check, which does not change your score.
  4. Amex answers yes or no. If yes, it shows the welcome offer attached to the approval.
  5. You choose. If you accept the card, Amex then submits a hard inquiry. If you walk away, no hard inquiry is made.

For the Platinum Card, One Mile at a Time describes the same order in November 2026: Amex shows your offer first, and no hard inquiry happens unless you accept.

Limits of the notice

Situation What happens
New to Amex, applying for a personal card The notice is offered
Logged in to an existing Amex account Not offered, per The Points Guy and One Mile at a Time
Existing Amex cardholder Not needed in most cases; Amex generally does not run a hard inquiry on current customers, per Bankrate and The Points Guy
Business cards Not offered

Accepting the card still has an effect. Upgraded Points says the hard inquiry can lower a score by a few points for a time. For what that does to a score, see does applying for a travel card hurt your credit score.

Whether Amex can still decline an application after you accept is not confirmed. No source read addresses it.

Approval is not the same as the welcome offer

Two separate things are decided: whether you get the card, and whether you get a welcome offer.

  • Welcome offers vary by applicant and change over time. The amount you are shown may differ from the highest advertised figure.
  • Amex generally gives a welcome offer on a given card once per lifetime. A pop-up during the application tells you if you are not eligible for the offer, and you can stop there. See the once-per-lifetime rule.
  • For ways to see your own offer before deciding, see how to check your Platinum offer.

If you are declined

CNBC Select says a declined applicant can call Amex and ask for the decision to be reconsidered. Upgraded Points suggests checking your credit reports for errors, paying down balances and building a longer history before trying again.

Who should skip it

Hold off on this card for now if:

  • You are new to credit or have had a credit card for under a year.
  • You have recent late payments or high balances on other cards.
  • A hard inquiry would matter soon, for example before a mortgage application.
  • Paying an $895 annual fee would strain your budget.

Whether the card is worth its fee is a separate question from whether you would be approved. See what the credits are worth.

Quick answers

What is the minimum credit score for the Amex Platinum? Amex does not publish one. Review sites suggest good to excellent credit, with estimates from 670 to 720.

Is there a minimum income for the Amex Platinum? None is published. One guide says $50,000 or more may help, but that is an estimate, not an Amex rule.

Does checking for approval with Amex cause a hard inquiry? Not under Apply With Confidence. Amex runs a soft check first, and a hard inquiry follows only if you accept the card.
