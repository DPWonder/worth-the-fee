What are the Sapphire Reserve credits really worth if you only count the ones you would use anyway?

Facts checked November 22, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

At face value the Sapphire Reserve's recurring credits add up to about $2,384 a year against a $795 fee, before counting the DashPass membership and the Global Entry credit. Count only what you would have paid for anyway and most people land far lower, often between $300 and $900, so the card pays for itself only for those who already spend in several of its categories.

Why face value is the wrong number

A credit is worth its full amount only if it replaces money you were going to spend anyway. Three things shrink it:

  • Narrow lists. The dining credit works at a set list of restaurants, not at any restaurant. The Edit credit works at a set collection of hotels, and needs a prepaid stay of two nights.
  • Use it or lose it. The half-year and monthly credits do not roll over. A missed month of Lyft or DoorDash is gone.
  • Spending you would not have done. A hotel in The Edit can cost more than the hotel you would have picked.

Add up your own number

Go down the list and write a dollar figure next to each line. Use last year's real spending, not what you hope to do.

  1. Travel credit. If you spend $300 a year on any travel, write $300. Most cardholders can.
  2. The Edit. Write $250 for each two-night prepaid stay you would book at one of these hotels at the price Chase shows. If you are not sure, write $0.
  3. Dining. Check the Exclusive Tables list for your city. Write $150 per half-year only if you would eat at one of those restaurants anyway.
  4. StubHub. Write what you spent on resale tickets last year, up to $150 per half.
  5. DoorDash. Write $15 for each month you already order restaurant delivery, and $10 or $20 for each month you already order groceries or convenience items there.
  6. Lyft and Peloton. Write $10 for each month you already pay for them.
  7. Apple. Write what you pay now for Apple TV and Apple Music. If you pay for neither, write $0.
  8. Global Entry. Write $30.

Add the lines up and subtract $795.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone whose honest total is under $795 and who does not value the lounges or the higher earning rates enough to cover the gap.
  • Anyone who does not want to track monthly and half-year deadlines.
  • Anyone who carries a balance. Interest will outweigh any credit.
  • Light travelers. The Sapphire Preferred at $95 asks for much less.

Quick answers

Does the $300 travel credit alone justify the fee? No. It brings the cost down to $495. The remaining credits, points and lounges have to cover that.

Do the credits roll over if I miss one? No. Monthly and half-year credits expire at the end of each period.

Are all the credits permanent? No. Lyft ends 9/30/2027, StubHub and Peloton end 12/31/2027, and the DoorDash benefits run through 12/31/2029 unless Chase extends them.
