How do purchase protection and extended warranty work on the Chase Sapphire Preferred?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Purchase protection on the Sapphire Preferred covers a new item against theft or damage for 120 days from purchase, up to $500 per item and $50,000 per account. Extended warranty adds one year to a manufacturer's warranty of three years or less, up to $10,000 per item and $50,000 per account.

What purchase protection pays

The benefit administrator chooses to repair the item, replace it or pay you back. The payment is capped at what you charged or at $500, whichever is lower. A $1,200 laptop stolen in month two would bring $500 at most.

Upgraded Points lists these exclusions: used items, antiques and collectibles; vehicles; computer software; medical equipment; perishables; cash, gift cards and tickets; items bought for resale or commercial use; items that "mysteriously disappear"; items in the hands of a shipper or airline; jewelry and watches in checked baggage; and items never delivered. The Points Guy also names animals.

What extended warranty pays

Chase's guide says the benefit adds one year to a United States manufacturer's warranty of three years or less. Cover ends four years after the purchase date.

The payment is the lowest of the repair cost, the replacement cost or the amount charged, up to $10,000 per item.

Chase's guide excludes motorized vehicles and their parts, items bought for resale or for professional or commercial use, rented or leased items, computer software, medical equipment, and used or pre-owned items.

How to claim

  1. Report the loss or failure to the benefit administrator within 90 days. Upgraded Points gives the phone number as 888-320-9656.
  2. For theft, file a police report. Upgraded Points says within 48 hours.
  3. Return the claim form and papers within 120 days.

Chase and Upgraded Points name these papers: the card statement, the itemized store receipt, proof of any other payment method, the original warranty for a warranty claim, a repair estimate or paid repair bill, and a police, fire or insurance report for theft or damage.

Where sources disagree

  • Lost items. The Points Guy says the cover applies to items that are lost, damaged or stolen. Upgraded Points says theft and accidental damage, and lists items that mysteriously disappear as excluded. Do not count on cover for an item you misplaced.
  • Warranty length. Chase says warranties of three years or less qualify. Upgraded Points says under three years.

Not confirmed: whether a gift is covered for the person who receives it, whether the benefit pays only after other insurance, and any yearly cap beyond the $50,000 per account.

Who should skip relying on it

  • Anyone buying items well over $500, such as a high-end phone or laptop. Most of the loss stays with you.
  • Anyone buying used or refurbished goods.
  • Anyone who does not keep receipts and warranty papers.

Quick answers

Does Sapphire Preferred purchase protection cover a cracked phone screen? Accidental damage in the first 120 days is covered, up to $500 per item. After 120 days it is not.

Does the extended warranty apply to a five-year warranty? No. Chase's guide limits it to warranties of three years or less.

Is there a fee or sign-up for either benefit? No sign-up is named in any source read. The item has to be paid for with the card or its points.
