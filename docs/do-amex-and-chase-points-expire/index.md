# Do Amex and Chase points expire, and what happens if you close the card?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/do-amex-and-chase-points-expire/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Amex Membership Rewards points and Chase points do not expire while the card account stays open and in good standing. They are lost when you close the last card tied to that points balance, so use them or move them before you close it; sources do not agree on whether you get any days of grace afterward.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The rules side by side

| | Amex points (Membership Rewards) | Chase points |
|---|---|---|
| Expire with time or inactivity | No | No |
| Close one card, keep another in the same points balance | Points stay | Points stay if you move them to the open card first |
| Close the last card holding the points | Points forfeited | Points forfeited |
| Days to redeem after closing | Sources disagree: none, or 30 | Sources disagree: none stated by Chase, or 30 |
| Late payment | Points earned in that billing period can be forfeited; a $35 fee reinstates them after you pay | All points lost if the minimum payment is 60 days late |
| Issuer closes the account for misuse | Points forfeited | Points forfeited |

Sources: Chase's own guide, AwardWallet (February 2026 for Chase), The Points Guy, View from the Wing and One Mile at a Time (October 2026 for Amex).

## Chase: what the bank says

Chase's guide says points do not expire as long as the card account is open. In Chase's words: "Inactivity won't cause your Chase points to disappear from your account."

On closing, the same guide says that if you close the card before redeeming or transferring, you no longer have those points. It adds that points are forfeited if Chase closes the account for missed payments, program misuse or other reasons in the agreement.

Before closing a Chase card you can:

- Move the points to another of your own Chase cards that earns the same points.
- Move them to a household member's card. AwardWallet says a member of your household; The Points Guy says a spouse or domestic partner.
- Transfer them to an airline or hotel partner. That is final, and the points then follow that program's rules.
- Change the card to a no-fee Chase card instead of closing it. The Points Guy notes that if the new card cannot transfer to partners, you should transfer first.

## Amex: what changed in October 2026

Amex points do not expire from inactivity, per AwardWallet, which quotes Amex's FAQ that points can be forfeited as the program terms describe.

Until October 2026, a new Amex card joined your existing points balance on its own. View from the Wing and One Mile at a Time report that Amex changed its terms that month: a newly opened US card now gets its own rewards account, and the balances combine only after the accounts are linked. Existing cards were not changed.

Why it matters when closing a card: View from the Wing reads Amex's terms as forfeiting a rewards account's balance when the last card linked to that account is canceled. Another Amex card under the same login does not protect the points unless it is linked to the same rewards account. Its example: closing a new, unlinked Platinum Card could forfeit the points earned on it even though a Gold Card stays open.

Before closing an Amex card:

- Check the links under Manage Connections on the Amex site or app, or call the number on the card.
- Confirm the combined balance shows on a card you are keeping.
- Or redeem or transfer the points first.

Amex's terms document did not load as readable text, so the forfeiture wording was not confirmed on Amex's own page.

## Where sources disagree

- **Days after closing an Amex card.** AwardWallet says you have 30 days to use the points. The Points Guy (2024) says they are forfeited at once when your only Membership Rewards card closes, with a 30-day window only if you keep another Amex card that does not earn these points. View from the Wing (October 2026) describes forfeiture on canceling the last linked card, a 90-day window for cardholders with a New York billing address, and up to 30 days for a last linked Corporate Card.
- **Days after closing a Chase card.** Chase's guide mentions no window. AwardWallet and The Points Guy say there are 30 days if the account is in good standing.

The safe reading for both: treat the points as gone on the day the last card closes.

## Points you have already transferred

Miles and hotel points that have reached an airline or hotel account are no longer the bank's. Closing the card does not remove them. They can expire under that program's own rules; Hyatt points, for example, expire after 24 months with no qualifying activity.

A transfer made only to rescue points locks them into one program; see [Amex or Chase points, which are worth more](/amex-points-vs-chase-points-which-are-worth-more/).

## Who does not need to worry, and who should skip closing

- If you keep at least one card on the same points balance open and pay on time, nothing here applies.
- If the annual fee is the reason, ask about a lower-fee card in the same program before closing.
- If you opened an Amex card in or after October 2026, check the link before closing any older card.
- If you would transfer points in a hurry with no trip in mind, compare [which Chase partners](/chase-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/) and [which Amex partners](/amex-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/) hold their value.

## Quick answers

**Do Chase points expire?**
No, not while the card account is open. Chase says inactivity does not remove them.

**Do Amex points expire?**
No, not from inactivity. They can be forfeited when the last linked card closes or after a late payment.

**Do I lose miles already moved to an airline when I close the card?**
No. They sit in the airline's program and follow its expiry rules.
