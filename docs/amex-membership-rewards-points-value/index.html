What is an Amex Membership Rewards point actually worth?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

An Amex Membership Rewards point is worth about 0.6 cents when used against card charges, 0.7 cents at shopping checkouts and on most Amex Travel hotels, 1 cent on flights booked through Amex Travel, and roughly 1 to 2 cents or more when moved to an airline partner and used well. The point has no single value; it depends on how you spend it.

Where sources disagree

Gift cards. The Points Guy gives a range of 0.5 to 1 cent. CNBC Select says gift cards are generally 1 cent, with some brands at 0.7 or 0.85 cents and American Express gift cards at 0.5 cents. point.me gives about 0.7 cents. The rate is set brand by brand, so check the points price of the exact card.

Transfer partners. This is an estimate, not a rate. The Points Guy values a point at 2.0 cents (April 2026). One Mile at a Time says 1.7 cents. point.me says about 1.7 cents for a standard transfer and 3 to 5 cents for premium-cabin awards. CNBC Select notes that transfers to Marriott Bonvoy and Choice Privileges are typically under 1 cent, and that Delta SkyMiles average about 1.2 cents.

Number of partners. CNBC Select counts 21 (18 airlines and 3 hotel programs). point.me counts 20. The list changes.

How transfers work

Most partners take points at 1 to 1. Some differ:

Partner Ratio (Amex points to partner points)
Most airline partners 1 : 1
Hilton Honors 1 : 2
Aeromexico Rewards 1 : 1.6
Cathay Pacific, Emirates Skywards 5 : 4
JetBlue TrueBlue 250 : 200

A higher ratio does not mean higher value. Two Hilton points at The Points Guy's 0.4 cents each come to 0.8 cents per Amex point.

Transfers to US airlines carry a fee. Thrifty Traveler and point.me give it as $0.0006 per point (0.06 cents), capped at $99, on transfers to Delta and JetBlue. That is $30 on 50,000 points. The Points Guy and CNBC Select print larger per-point figures (0.6 and 0.5 cents), which do not fit the $99 cap; the fee shown on Amex's transfer screen is the one that applies.

A transfer partner value above 1 cent exists only if you find an award seat you would have paid cash for.

A worked example

Take the 24,000 points that $6,000 of supermarket spending earns on the Gold Card:

  • As a statement credit: $144.
  • As a flight through Amex Travel: $240.
  • Moved to an airline at 1.7 cents: $408, if a suitable award is found.

The same points are worth almost three times as much at the top of that list as at the bottom.

Which number to use for your own sums

  • If you would take cash or a statement credit, use 0.6 cents.
  • If you would book flights through Amex Travel, use 1 cent.
  • If you already book award flights through airline programs and have flexible dates, the published valuations of 1.7 to 2 cents are the figures to test. They are not guaranteed.

Published valuations describe what an experienced user can get. They are not a cash price.

Who should skip a Membership Rewards card

  • People who want cash back. At 0.6 cents per point, a card that earns 4 points per dollar returns 2.4%, and a card that earns 1 point returns 0.6%.
  • People who will not learn an airline program or search for award seats.
  • People with fixed travel dates and one home airline that is not a transfer partner.
  • People who would mostly redeem for gift cards or checkout purchases.

Points can also come from shopping portals; see Rakuten paid in Amex points.

Quick answers

What is the cash value of an Amex point? About 0.6 cents when used to cover card charges, according to The Points Guy, CNBC Select and point.me.

Is a point really worth 2 cents? Only as an estimate for transfers to travel partners. The Points Guy uses 2.0 cents and One Mile at a Time 1.7 cents. Most other uses give 1 cent or less.

Is there a fee to transfer points to an airline? For US airlines such as Delta and JetBlue, yes. Thrifty Traveler gives it as 0.06 cents per point, up to $99.
