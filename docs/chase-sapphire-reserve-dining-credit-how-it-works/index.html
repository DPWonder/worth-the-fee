How does the Sapphire Reserve dining credit (Exclusive Tables) work, and which restaurants count?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Sapphire Reserve dining credit pays back up to $150 from January to June and up to $150 from July to December when you pay with the card at a restaurant in the Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables list on OpenTable. Only those restaurants count, reported at roughly 360 to 400 in about 50 US cities and metro areas, so the credit is worth nothing in a city without one.

Points The Points Guy reports 3x on the whole bill, credited part included

Chase's own example: $80 at one restaurant in February and $70 at another in May fill the first half-year's $150.

Which restaurants count

Only restaurants in the Exclusive Tables program. Chase does not publish a count and says the list can change.

  • The Points Guy reports figures from 362 to 397 restaurants, in about 50 US cities and metro areas listed by OpenTable.
  • Thrifty Traveler counted 51 locations and found three participating restaurants in the Twin Cities.
  • Chase has city guides for New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, San Francisco, Philadelphia, Houston, Las Vegas and San Diego.
  • Restaurants in OpenTable's wider Visa Dining Collection do not count unless they carry the Sapphire Reserve badge.

The current list is at opentable.com/sapphire-reserve-exclusive-tables, or through the benefits section of the Chase account.

Do you have to book through OpenTable?

The credit comes from paying with the card at a listed restaurant. The Points Guy states that a reservation made through Exclusive Tables is not required.

The program also holds back prime-time tables for cardholders. To see and book those, you add the Reserve to an OpenTable account and pass an eligibility check; Chase's terms say new cardmembers may wait up to four business days. Whether that link is also needed for the credit itself is not confirmed. Linking the card costs nothing, so doing it removes the doubt.

What does not count

Chase's terms exclude:

  • Gift cards and merchandise.
  • Orders through delivery platforms.
  • Purchases run through a third-party payment processor. The charge must be identifiable as made directly with the restaurant.

Two gray areas. The Points Guy reports cases where a restaurant gift card did trigger the credit, which conflicts with the terms; do not rely on it. Thrifty Traveler says takeout is unclear and that some people report credits posting with Apple Pay or Google Pay.

When the credit posts

Source Stated timing
Chase's terms Within 3 business days, up to 4 weeks
The Points Guy One or two billing cycles
Thrifty Traveler Six to eight weeks

Each period ends at 11:59 PM Eastern on its last day. A meal on June 29 or December 30 that the restaurant processes late can land in the next period. Chase can reverse a credit if the charge is refunded or changed, or if the account closes within 90 days.

What it is worth

These are mostly sit-down restaurants where a dinner for two can pass $150. The Points Guy's writer spent about $196 on one visit. So the usual result is $150 off a meal that costs more, twice a year.

The last row is a cost of $100, not a gain of $300.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone living far from the listed cities. Check the list before counting a dollar of this credit.
  • Anyone who rarely eats at restaurants in this price range.
  • Anyone who mostly orders delivery. Delivery platforms are excluded.
  • Anyone who will not track two deadlines a year.

For a different dining credit with a wider restaurant list, see how the Amex Platinum Resy credit works.

Quick answers

Does any restaurant on OpenTable count? No. Only restaurants in the Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables list count.

Does unused credit from January to June carry into July? No. Each half-year's $150 expires at the end of its period.

Does delivery from a listed restaurant count? Not through a delivery platform. Chase's terms exclude those orders.
