Is the Rakuten browser extension safe, and what data does it collect?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The extension is published by Rakuten itself and listed in the official Chrome and Firefox stores. It collects the addresses of pages you visit, whether or not you are using it at that moment, plus shopping details such as searches, cart items and order totals. Uninstalling it stops that collection.

No independent security audit of the extension was found, so that part of "safe" is not confirmed. What can be checked is what Rakuten says the extension does.

What it collects

Rakuten's privacy notice for the extension was updated on March 13, 2026. It says the extension collects information on the pages you visit "whether you are interacting with it or not." The notice lists:

  • URLs of the pages you visit and general information about those pages.
  • Product searches and search results.
  • Items you add to a cart.
  • Products, coupons and offers you view or click.
  • Date and time of use, browser type and version, and device type.
  • On order confirmation pages at certain stores: what you ordered and the order total.

It also covers some web-based generative AI apps. There the extension looks for the names or domains of Rakuten's partner stores in the AI's answer, so it can show an offer. The notice says Rakuten does not collect or store your chat history.

What the browser stores show

In the Chrome Web Store, the developer declares that the extension handles three kinds of data: web history, user activity and website content. It also declares that the data is not sold to third parties outside approved use cases, is not used for purposes unrelated to the extension's core function, and is not used to judge creditworthiness.

The Firefox listing shows the permissions asked for: access to browser tabs, access to browser activity during navigation, and access to your data for all websites. Notifications are optional. This was seen in a search summary of the listing.

What Rakuten uses it for

  • Showing offers, including by email.
  • Confirming cash back faster. Rakuten calls this Instant Cash Back and says it is not available at every store.
  • Reminders about items left in a cart, and coupon suggestions at checkout.
  • Internal testing based on shopping behavior.

When you activate an offer, a cookie is set through an affiliate network so the store can credit the order to Rakuten. The notice and Rakuten's terms both say this can overwrite affiliate cookies from other companies.

For who else receives data, the notice points to Rakuten's main privacy policy, which names affiliate networks, stores, advertising providers and other Rakuten Group companies. How long extension data is kept is not stated in the notice.

You do not need the extension to earn. Starting each purchase from the Rakuten website or app also tracks it. The extension mainly saves that step.

Who should skip it

  • You do not want any company to hold a record of the pages you visit. Use the website for each purchase instead.
  • You already run another rewards or coupon extension. Rakuten says a competing extension can stop its tracking, and asks members to disable or uninstall others.
  • You share a browser profile with other people. Their browsing would be collected under your account.

Quick answers

Does the Rakuten extension track all the sites I visit? Rakuten's notice says it collects information on the pages you visit, including URLs, even when idle.

Does Rakuten read my AI chats? Its notice says it looks for partner store names in the AI's answer and does not collect or store chat history.

How do I stop the collection? Uninstall or disable the extension. You can still earn by starting at the Rakuten website.
