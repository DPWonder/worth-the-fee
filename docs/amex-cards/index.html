Other Amex cards: every short answer on one page

Each answer links to its full page, with sources and the date it was checked. This is information, not financial advice.

Blue Cash Preferred or Blue Cash Everyday: at what grocery spend does the annual fee pay off?

The Blue Cash Preferred's $95 annual fee pays off against the no-fee Blue Cash Everyday once you spend more than about $3,167 a year at US supermarkets, which is roughly $264 a month. The most the grocery rate can put you ahead is $85 a year, because the 6% rate stops at $6,000 of grocery spending.

Is the Amex Gold Card worth its annual fee if you cook at home most nights?

For a home cook the Amex Gold Card can cover its $325 annual fee, but only if you spend a lot at US supermarkets or you would use its monthly credits anyway. On grocery points alone, valued at 1 cent each, it takes about $8,125 of supermarket spending a year to pay the fee back.

Does the Amex Gold Card have cell phone protection?

No. The consumer American Express Gold Card is not on Amex's list of cards with cell phone protection, and its own benefits page does not show the benefit. What it does have is purchase protection, which can cover a phone bought with the card for the first 90 days.

What is an Amex Membership Rewards point actually worth?

An Amex Membership Rewards point is worth about 0.6 cents when used against card charges, 0.7 cents at shopping checkouts and on most Amex Travel hotels, 1 cent on flights booked through Amex Travel, and roughly 1 to 2 cents or more when moved to an airline partner and used well. The point has no single value; it depends on how you spend it.

What is the Amex "once per lifetime" welcome offer rule, and how do you know if you are still eligible?

The "once per lifetime" rule means American Express generally gives the welcome offer on a card only once per person: if you have or have had that card, you may not get the offer again. You find out for certain during the application, where Amex shows a notice if no welcome offer is available to you and lets you withdraw before the application is processed.

Delta SkyMiles Gold, Platinum or Reserve Amex: which one fits someone who flies Delta 4 to 8 times a year?

At 4 to 8 Delta flights a year, the Gold card ($150 after a free first year) fits most people who check a bag, because the bag benefit is the same on all three cards. The Platinum ($350) makes sense mainly if you will use its yearly companion certificate, and the Reserve ($650) mainly if you want Delta Sky Club visits.

How many Delta flights does it take for the SkyMiles Gold Amex checked-bag benefit to cover the annual fee?

Four one-way Delta flights with one checked bag, which is two round trips, cover the Delta SkyMiles Gold Amex's $150 annual fee for a solo traveler. Two people checking a bag each on the same reservation cover it in one round trip.

Hilton Honors Surpass or Aspire: which Amex is worth it for two or three Hilton stays a year?

For two or three Hilton stays a year, the Surpass ($150) is the easier card to earn back, because its Hilton credits can cover most of the fee on those stays. The Aspire ($550) comes out ahead only if you will use its yearly free night at a hotel you would otherwise pay well for, plus its resort or flight credits.

Is the Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant Amex worth its annual fee for an occasional Marriott guest?

For an occasional Marriott guest, the Bonvoy Brilliant's $650 annual fee is worth it only if two things are true: you eat at restaurants every month, so the $300 dining credit is used in full, and you will use the yearly free night at a hotel that would cost you $350 or more. The elite status on its own will not earn back the fee on a few stays a year.
