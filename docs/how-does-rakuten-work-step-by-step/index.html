How does Rakuten work, step by step?

Facts checked November 23, 20264 min readHow we check facts

The short answer

You create a free account, click through Rakuten to a store, and buy as usual. The store pays Rakuten a commission for the sale, Rakuten passes part of it to you as cash back, and confirmed cash back is paid four times a year once you have about $5.

Step 1: Create an account

Rakuten says joining is free. You sign up with an email and password, or with a Facebook, Google or Apple account. Its terms say members must be at least 18 and live in the 50 states or Washington D.C., and each person may have one account.

Step 2: Start a shopping trip

A "shopping trip" is Rakuten's name for the click that takes you from Rakuten to a store. Each trip gets a unique number, which Rakuten uses to verify the purchase with the store. Rakuten's help page says purchases earn cash back "only if you start a Shopping Trip with Rakuten."

There are four ways to start one.

Way to shop How the trip starts Where it works
Rakuten.com Pick the store on the site and click through Computer or phone browser
Browser extension Click the activate button when it appears on a store's site Desktop Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari; Safari on iPhone
Mobile app Tap the store in the app iOS and Android
In a physical store Link a payment card, add the store's offer, pay with that card Participating stores and restaurants

A pop-up confirms that cash back is active. You need a new trip for every purchase. Rakuten's pages differ on how many stores take part: one says 3,500+, another over 3,700. For how the two main tools differ, see the app and the extension compared. Card linking has its own rules; see how in-store cash back works and which cards can be linked.

Step 3: Buy at the store

You check out on the store's own website and pay the store, not Rakuten. Rakuten's example: spend $100 at a store offering 5% and you earn $5. Taxes and shipping do not earn cash back, and each store lists its excluded items. Ad blockers, a coupon code from another site, or checking out in a different tab can break the tracking.

Step 4: The store pays Rakuten, and Rakuten shares it

The store reports the order to Rakuten and pays it a commission for sending the shopper. Rakuten keeps a portion and gives the rest to you as cash back. What share Rakuten keeps is not confirmed; its page says only that it shares part. The business side is covered in how Rakuten makes money.

Step 5: Processing, pending, confirmed

Status What it means Typical time, per Rakuten
Processing Rakuten is waiting for the store to send the order details 24 to 48 hours
Pending The store has reported the order; its return period is running 3 to 17 weeks overall; 3 to 14 weeks for online and in-store shopping
Confirmed The amount is in your confirmed balance Included in the next payment
Ineligible The order did not qualify Returns, exclusions, coupon codes not found on Rakuten

Travel is slower: Rakuten says it is typically confirmed 6 to 17 weeks after the trip is complete. See how long a purchase takes to show up, and if nothing appears, what to do when cash back did not track.

Step 6: Get paid

Rakuten pays on February 15, May 15, August 15 and November 15. Each payment covers the cash back confirmed before that date.

The minimum is about $5. Rakuten's help page says $5 or more; its terms say $5.01. A smaller balance carries over to the next date.

Payment can be by PayPal, check, Bilt points or Samsung Cash. Eligible American Express card members can take Membership Rewards points instead. Worked dates are in when Rakuten pays.

Who should skip it

  • You want the saving at checkout. Rakuten pays months later; a lower price or a coupon is money today.
  • You do not want a company recording where you shop. See what the extension collects.
  • You always use coupon codes from other sites. Codes not listed on Rakuten can void the cash back.
  • You shop online once or twice a year. The balance may sit under the minimum for a long time.

Quick answers

Do you pay Rakuten anything? No. Rakuten says joining is free. Its income is the commission stores pay for each sale.

Do you have to click through Rakuten every time? Yes. Each purchase needs its own shopping trip, started from the site, app or extension.

How often does Rakuten pay? Four times a year: February 15, May 15, August 15 and November 15, with about $5 confirmed.
