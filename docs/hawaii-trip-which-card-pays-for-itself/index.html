For one week in Hawaii, which travel card comes closest to paying for itself?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Counting only what one Hawaii trip returns, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the only one of the three that covers its own fee: a $100 hotel credit against a $95 fee. The Sapphire Reserve comes back about $245 short and the Amex Platinum about $415 short, so both need their other credits to break even.

The assumptions

Change any of these and the sums change.

  • Two travelers, one trip in the year, flying from the mainland in economy.
  • One checked bag each, both ways, at $45 a bag. That is $180 in bag fees.
  • Five hotel nights, booked as one prepaid stay through the card's travel site, at a hotel in that card's program.
  • Counted: statement credits the trip itself triggers.
  • Not counted: welcome offers, which vary by applicant and change; points earned; lounge visits; insurance; credits for things outside the trip, such as dining or streaming.

The worked example

Sapphire Preferred Sapphire Reserve Amex Platinum
Annual fee $95 $795 $895
Hotel credit used $100 (Chase Travel hotel stay) $250 (The Edit, prepaid, two nights or more) $300 (Fine Hotels + Resorts or The Hotel Collection, prepaid)
Travel or airline credit used None $300 (any travel purchase) $180 of the $200 airline fee credit, for the bag fees
Total credits from the trip $100 $550 $480
Credits minus fee +$5 -$245 -$415
Bag fees still paid by you $180 $180 (the $300 credit is already used in full) $0 if both bags are on the one chosen airline

Each premium card's hotel program also gives a $100 property credit and daily breakfast for two. If you would spend that $100 at the hotel anyway, the gaps shrink to about -$145 for the Reserve and -$315 for the Platinum.

What sits behind each number

Sapphire Preferred. The $100 credit applies each account anniversary year to hotel stays bought through Chase Travel. The card has no bag fee benefit and no lounge access.

Sapphire Reserve. The $300 travel credit applies to any purchase the card categorizes as travel, so airfare or the hotel will use it in full. The Edit credit is up to $250 per prepaid booking with a two-night minimum, up to $500 a calendar year. Chase's travel guides name Hawaii hotels in The Edit, including The Royal Hawaiian. The full list was not read.

Amex Platinum. The hotel credit is up to $300 per half-year, so one stay uses one of the two. The airline fee credit works on one airline chosen in advance from Alaska, American, Delta, JetBlue, Southwest and United. Whether it applies to a bag fee on a Hawaiian-operated flight is not confirmed.

What the trip does not show

Three things the table leaves out can matter more than the credits.

  • Hotel price. The Reserve and Platinum credits only work at higher-priced hotels in each program. Booking a much dearer room to collect $300 costs more than it returns.
  • Lounges. Both premium cards open lounges at Honolulu and Maui and none at Kona or Lihue. See Hawaii lounges by card.
  • Points. All three earn extra points on travel booked through their own travel sites. The value depends on what you spend.

How the premium cards close the gap

Card Gap after the trip Other yearly credits that could close it
Sapphire Reserve $245 A second $250 Edit stay; up to $300 dining; up to $300 StubHub; Lyft, DoorDash, Peloton and Apple credits
Amex Platinum $415 The second $300 hotel credit; up to $400 Resy; up to $300 digital entertainment; up to $200 Uber Cash; other listed credits

A credit only counts if you would have bought the thing anyway. That question is worked through in what the Platinum credits are worth and Preferred or Reserve for a light traveler.

Who should skip which card

  • One trip a year and no use for dining, ride or streaming credits: skip both premium cards. The sums above do not close.
  • Condo or vacation rental instead of a hotel: none of the three hotel credits applies as assumed here.
  • Flying home from Kona or Lihue and hoping for a lounge: neither premium card opens one there.

Quick answers

Which card pays for itself on one Hawaii trip? On trip credits alone, only the Sapphire Preferred: $100 back against a $95 fee.

How far short is the Amex Platinum on one trip? About $415 under these assumptions: $480 in trip credits against an $895 fee.

Do any of these cards include free checked bags? No. The Platinum's airline fee credit and the Reserve's travel credit can repay bag fees, within their rules.
