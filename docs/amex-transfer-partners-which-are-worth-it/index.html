Which Amex transfer partners are worth using, and which rarely are?

Facts checked November 20, 20265 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Among Amex's roughly 20 transfer partners, Avianca LifeMiles, Virgin Atlantic, Air Canada Aeroplan, Flying Blue, ANA and the Avios programs are valued above the 1 cent a point is worth on Amex Travel flights. Hilton, Marriott, Choice and Emirates come out below 1 cent per Amex point, and Cathay Pacific and JetBlue sit close to it.

The test: does it beat 1 cent?

An Amex point buys 1 cent of airfare through Amex Travel; see what a Membership Rewards point is worth. A transfer makes sense only when the award returns more than that after taxes and fees. Transfers cannot be reversed.

Two things lower the return before you start. Some partners take points at worse than 1 to 1. And transfers to US airlines (Delta, JetBlue) carry a fee of 0.06 cents per point, up to $99.

Partners that tend to be worth it

  • ANA Mileage Club. A round trip between the US and Europe on partner airlines is 55,000 miles in economy and 100,000 in business, per AwardWallet. Transfers take about two days, and ANA passes on surcharges from some airlines.
  • Avianca LifeMiles. No fuel surcharges on partner awards, per AwardWallet and Frequent Miler.
  • Virgin Atlantic. Economy between the US and London from 6,000 points one way, plus taxes and fees that The Points Guy puts near $165. It also books ANA flights; see points to Japan.
  • Flying Blue. From 25,000 miles one way in economy and 60,000 in business between the US and Europe; see points to Europe.
  • Iberia. Off-peak business class from New York or Boston to Madrid at 40,500 Avios one way, per The Points Guy.

Partners that rarely are

  • Hilton, Marriott and Choice. AwardWallet says hotel transfers rarely return more than about 1 cent per Amex point. Hilton's 1 to 2 ratio still comes to 0.8 cents.
  • Emirates. The 5 to 4 ratio and high surcharges on its own flights.
  • Delta. Prices move with the cash fare. AwardWallet found Los Angeles to Paris at 68,000 miles round trip in economy.

Where sources disagree

  • Aeromexico. The arithmetic above gives 1.28 cents. AwardWallet ranks it among the weaker airline partners and cites economy awards to Europe at 40,000 to 80,000 points.
  • Aer Lingus. Valued with the other Avios programs, but AwardWallet does not recommend transfers to it for now because of booking problems.
  • Iberia's price. One AwardWallet page still prints 34,000 Avios for the Madrid business award; The Points Guy and a newer AwardWallet page say 40,500.
  • Partner count. CNBC Select counts 21, point.me 20. AwardWallet's table has 16 airline programs and four hotel programs.
  • Bonuses. AwardWallet lists 30% to Virgin Atlantic through November 30, 10% to Avianca through December 16 and 35% to Marriott through December 10, 2026. Not confirmed on an Amex page.

Who should skip transfers

  • People who want cash or simple bookings. Amex Travel flights at 1 cent need no award search.
  • People who mostly want hotel nights. Amex has no Hyatt transfer.
  • Delta-only flyers expecting 2 cents a point.

Quick answers

Which Amex partners are not 1 to 1? Hilton (1 to 2), Aeromexico (1 to 1.6), Cathay Pacific and Emirates (5 to 4) and JetBlue (250 to 200).

Is there a fee to transfer Amex points? Only to US airlines such as Delta and JetBlue: 0.06 cents per point, capped at $99 per transfer.

How long do transfers take? Most are immediate, per AwardWallet. ANA takes about two days, and Iberia, Qatar, Singapore and Cathay Pacific up to a day.
