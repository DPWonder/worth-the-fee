Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables: what the Chase program is and how to book it

Facts checked November 26, 20265 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables is a Chase program on OpenTable that sets aside prime-time reservations and special events at selected restaurants for Sapphire Reserve cardholders. You book it by adding an eligible card to a free OpenTable account, passing an eligibility check, and reserving through OpenTable's website or app.

What the program is

Chase's benefit terms describe "exclusive access to primetime reservations and special events" at participating restaurants in select cities, offered through OpenTable. Chase's guide says the restaurants were chosen by The Infatuation, Chase and OpenTable together.

It is a booking benefit, not a discount. Nothing in Chase's terms lowers the bill, and you pay the restaurant as any guest would. Chase's guide calls making a reservation through the program complimentary, so there is no booking fee from Chase.

Which cards qualify

Chase's terms name three US-issued cards.

Card Reservation access Dining statement credit
Chase Sapphire Reserve Yes Yes
J.P. Morgan Reserve Yes Yes
Sapphire Reserve for Business Yes No
Chase Sapphire Preferred No No

Chase's guide says authorized users on an eligible account can also make reservations through the program. The Sapphire Preferred is not named in the terms. For how the two personal cards differ, see Sapphire Preferred or Reserve for a light traveler.

How to book

Chase's terms set out four steps.

  1. Go to OpenTable.com or open the OpenTable app.
  2. Sign in, or create a free OpenTable account.
  3. Complete the eligibility check that verifies the card.
  4. Add the eligible card to the OpenTable account.

After that, The Points Guy reports that a "Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables" banner appears on OpenTable's home page and leads to the held tables. Chase's account pages also link to OpenTable from the card's benefits section. A linked card does not need to be linked again unless it is removed.

OpenTable checks eligibility when the card is verified and then again once every 24 hours when you visit. A card that fails the first check can be stored and granted access later if it becomes eligible.

How it relates to the dining credit

The two share a restaurant list and a name, but they are separate benefits.

  • Exclusive Tables is about getting a table. It is used on OpenTable.
  • The dining credit is about paying. Chase says the credit comes from paying with the card at a restaurant in the program.

The Points Guy states that a reservation made through Exclusive Tables is not required for the credit. The same guide also says the card must first be connected to OpenTable to activate the credit, while Chase's own article ties the credit only to paying with the card at a listed restaurant. Whether the link is strictly required for the credit is not confirmed. The dollar rules, exclusions and posting times are on the dining credit page, and the yearly sums are on what the Reserve credits are worth.

Limits

  • Only listed restaurants. Other OpenTable restaurants are not part of the program.
  • Held tables are not guaranteed. Chase's terms say inventory not booked before the reservation day and time may be released to general OpenTable users. How many tables each restaurant holds is not confirmed.
  • Some bookings need the card. Where a restaurant asks for card details or prepayment, the terms say the eligible Chase card stored in the OpenTable account must be used.
  • Coverage is uneven. One Points Guy writer in a midsize city reported three nearby restaurants. Thrifty Traveler counted three in the Twin Cities.
  • Events. Prices and how often special events run are not confirmed.
  • Responsibility. The terms say OpenTable and Chase are not responsible for the dining experience itself.
  • Card status. If the eligible card is no longer stored in the OpenTable account, a new eligibility check may be required.

OpenTable's own program page could not be loaded on November 26, 2026, so the city list here rests on The Points Guy's reading of it.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone in a city with no listed restaurant. The program has nothing to book there except on trips.
  • Anyone who rarely eats at reservation-driven, higher-priced restaurants.
  • Anyone holding only the Sapphire Preferred. It is not an eligible card.
  • Anyone weighing the Reserve's annual fee on this benefit alone. Access to a table has no fixed dollar value; count the dining credit only if you would eat at a listed restaurant anyway.

Quick answers

Is Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables the same as the dining credit? No. Exclusive Tables is reservation access; the credit is a separate statement credit at the same restaurants.

Where do you book Exclusive Tables? On OpenTable's website or app, after adding an eligible Reserve card to your OpenTable account.

Does the Sapphire Reserve for Business get Exclusive Tables? Yes for reservation access. It does not get the dining statement credit.
