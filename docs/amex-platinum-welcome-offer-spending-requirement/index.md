# What counts toward the Amex Platinum welcome-offer spending requirement, and what does not?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/amex-platinum-welcome-offer-spending-requirement/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Ordinary purchases of goods and services count toward the Amex Platinum spending requirement, which is currently $12,000 in the first six months. The $895 annual fee, interest, gift cards, prepaid cards, person-to-person payments and other cash equivalents do not count, and refunds are taken back off your total.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The requirement in numbers

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Spending needed | $12,000 in eligible purchases |
| Time allowed | First six months of card membership |
| Average per month | $2,000 |
| Requirement before February 5, 2026 | $8,000 in six months |
| Annual fee (does not count) | $895 |

The points you receive vary by applicant. Read the requirement on your own offer screen, since terms can change.

## What counts and what does not

Amex's application terms would not load for this page, so the list of exclusions below comes from a January 2026 guide that quotes Amex's offer terms. Upgraded Points (April 2026) gives the same exclusions.

| Transaction | Counts? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday purchases: groceries, fuel, restaurants, travel, online shopping | Yes | General rule |
| Utility bills paid by card | Yes, reported | My Points Life |
| Tax payments through a card processor (fee of about 1.8% to 2%) | Yes, reported | My Points Life; One Mile at a Time says "if eligible" |
| Purchases by additional cardholders on your account | Yes | The Points Guy |
| The annual fee | No | One Mile at a Time, Upgraded Points |
| Interest, late fees and other fees | No | Amex terms as quoted |
| Gift cards | No | Amex terms as quoted; Upgraded Points |
| Buying or reloading prepaid cards | No | Amex terms as quoted |
| Person-to-person payments, including Amex Send & Split | No | Amex terms as quoted |
| Traveler's checks and other cash equivalents | No | Amex terms as quoted |
| Cash advances and balance transfers | No | My Points Life; Upgraded Points for balance transfers |

The terms exclude gift cards wherever they are bought, so do not plan on them.

## Refunds and statement credits

- **Refunds reduce your total.** The Points Guy states that a refunded amount is typically deducted from progress toward the requirement. A large return late in the six months can leave you short.
- **Refunds after the points arrive.** A search summary of that article says Amex can take back a welcome offer if a refund drops you under the requirement. Not confirmed on the page itself.
- **Card credits.** One guide reports that statement credits from card benefits, such as the airline fee credit, are treated as payments and do not reduce your progress. That is not confirmed by Amex.

A margin helps with both: aim a few hundred dollars over $12,000 so one return does not undo it.

## When the six months start, and when points arrive

- Amex's Gold Card terms, seen in a search result, count the six months from the date the account is opened. The Platinum terms were not read, so the exact start is not confirmed. Upgraded Points says the tracker in your account shows the deadline.
- The same Gold Card terms say points are credited within 8 to 12 weeks after the requirement is met. Upgraded Points reports that Membership Rewards welcome points tend to post within a few days. Neither timing is confirmed for the Platinum Card on an Amex page read today.
- A welcome offer tracker usually appears in the online account and the app, showing spending so far. Upgraded Points notes it can lag a few days and may not appear for every card.

## The sum to do first

Add up what you already put on cards in a normal six months.

| Your normal card spending per month | Six-month total | Reaches $12,000? |
|---|---|---|
| $1,500 | $9,000 | No, $3,000 short |
| $2,000 | $12,000 | Yes, with no margin for returns |
| $2,500 | $15,000 | Yes |

A planned large purchase can fill a gap. Spending you would not otherwise do is a cost, not a saving.

## Who should skip it

- Anyone whose normal card spending is well under $2,000 a month and who has no large planned purchase.
- Anyone who would carry a balance to get there. Interest does not count toward the requirement and can exceed the value of the points.
- Anyone who was counting on gift cards, prepaid cards or payments to friends to reach the total. Those are excluded.
- Anyone who has had the Platinum Card before. You may not be shown a welcome offer at all. See [the once-per-lifetime rule](/amex-once-per-lifetime-welcome-offer-rule/).

## Quick answers

**Does the $895 annual fee count toward the spending requirement?**
No. Fees are not purchases, so the annual fee does not count.

**Do gift cards count toward the Amex spending requirement?**
No. Amex's offer terms, as quoted by reviewers, exclude gift cards, prepaid cards and other cash equivalents.

**Does an authorized user's spending count?**
Yes. The Points Guy reports that purchases on additional cards count toward the primary cardmember's welcome offer.
