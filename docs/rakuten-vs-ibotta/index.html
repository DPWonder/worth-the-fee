Rakuten or Ibotta: how do they differ, and can you use both?

Facts checked November 26, 20265 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Rakuten is mainly an online shopping portal: you click through to a store and earn a percentage of the order, paid four times a year. Ibotta is mainly an app for item-by-item offers on groceries and everyday goods, claimed with a receipt or a linked store loyalty account and withdrawn once you have $20. You can hold both accounts; they overlap only on online shopping, where one click-through gets the credit.

Both fees come out of the rewards balance only. Ibotta says its fee is never taken from a personal bank account, and Rakuten's terms say its fee cannot make a balance negative.

What each one is for

Rakuten. A store pays Rakuten a commission for sending a shopper, and Rakuten passes part to the member. The reward is a percentage of the order, whatever the items are, subject to each store's exclusions. It also has card-linked offers in physical stores. The full sequence is on how Rakuten works, step by step.

Ibotta. Its help center describes offers tied to specific products. You add an offer to your list before shopping, buy the item, then prove the purchase in one of three ways:

  • Loyalty account. At retailers that support it, a linked store account matches purchases to offers with no receipt.
  • Paper receipt. For in-store purchases, a photo of the register receipt, submitted within 7 days. Digital and emailed receipts do not count.
  • Online shopping. A click-through from Ibotta's app, website or browser extension to the retailer, which works much like Rakuten.

How each one pays

Rakuten sends confirmed cash back on four fixed dates. Online and in-store cash back typically takes 3 to 14 weeks to be confirmed, and a balance under the minimum carries to the next date. Dates are worked through on when Rakuten pays.

Ibotta has no payment dates. Its help center lists three conditions before a first withdrawal: at least $20 in earnings, at least one redeemed offer, and 7 days since the first redeemed offer or gift card purchase. Funds then take 0 to 5 days depending on the method.

Ibotta method Detail from Ibotta's help center
Bank account Only banks that have opted in; linked in the app or on Ibotta.com
PayPal App only; usually within 2 hours, 1 to 3 business days in busy periods
Gift card Emailed within the hour; some cards have a $25 minimum purchase

Ibotta's online shopping offers also wait before they pay: 7 days after the transaction at qualifying retailers, with other retailers setting their own pending periods. Any withdrawal fee is not mentioned on the pages read.

Can you use both?

Holding both accounts breaks neither company's rules on the pages read. Where they meet depends on the purchase.

  • Groceries in a store. This is where Ibotta's product offers apply. Rakuten's cash back is set per store, not per grocery item.
  • Online orders at a store both list. Both work by a tracked click. Rakuten's help page says clicking a link from another coupon or rewards site after activating Rakuten "will disable Rakuten Cash Back." Plan on one program per order and compare the two rates first.
  • A linked card at a physical store plus an Ibotta receipt. Whether both pay on the same purchase is not confirmed; neither company's pages address it.

Minimum age and residency rules for Ibotta are not confirmed here, because its terms page did not load. Rakuten requires members to be 18 and to live in the 50 states or Washington D.C.

Who should skip it

  • Skip Ibotta if you will not add offers before shopping or keep paper receipts for a week. Its offers need both steps, or a linked loyalty account.
  • Skip Rakuten if you rarely order online. Its rewards come from store click-throughs and linked cards.
  • Skip both if a small balance would sit unused. Each has an inactivity fee that draws the balance down.
  • Skip both if you would buy items you did not plan to buy. An offer on an unneeded item is a cost.

Quick answers

Is Ibotta the same kind of service as Rakuten? Partly. Both pay cash back, but Ibotta centers on product offers and receipts; Rakuten centers on store click-throughs.

Which has the lower payout minimum? Rakuten, at about $5 on set dates. Ibotta requires $20 and pays when you ask.

Can both pay on the same online order? Do not count on it. Rakuten says a later click from another rewards site disables its cash back.
