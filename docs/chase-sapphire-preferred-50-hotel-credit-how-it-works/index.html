How does the Sapphire Preferred hotel credit work, and what bookings count?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Sapphire Preferred's $50 hotel credit no longer exists in that form: Chase doubled it to $100 on June 15, 2026. It is a statement credit of up to $100 each account anniversary year for hotel stays booked and paid for through Chase Travel with the card, and it does not apply to hotels booked anywhere else.

Before June 15, 2026 Now
Amount Up to $50 Up to $100
Period Account anniversary year Account anniversary year
Annual fee $95 $95
Activation None None

The Points Guy reports that existing cardholders who had already used $50 got another $50 from June 15. Some older articles still show the $50 figure.

What counts

  • A hotel stay bought through Chase Travel. Chase's terms name "hotel accommodation purchases" made through Chase Travel. A hotel booked on the hotel's own site or another travel site does not count.
  • Paid with the card. One Mile at a Time notes that you must choose to pay with the card at checkout. If you pay with points, the credit does not apply.
  • Prepaid. Chase's announcement says the credit needs a prepaid booking through Chase Travel. Chase's terms page does not repeat that word, so treat prepaid as the safe choice.
  • Any length of stay. Upgraded Points reports no minimum stay and no set list of hotels.

A stay that costs less than $100 uses part of the credit. Upgraded Points says the rest can go toward a later booking in the same account year. Its example is $79 on one stay and $21 on the next.

What does not count

  • Flights, rental cars and other travel in Chase Travel.
  • Hotels booked outside Chase Travel.
  • Whether vacation rentals in Chase Travel count is not confirmed.
  • Whether a booking made on an authorized user's card triggers the credit is not confirmed for this card.

When the year starts and ends

The credit follows your account, not the calendar. Chase's terms define the year as running from the account open date through the first statement date after the account anniversary, and then every 12 billing cycles. Unused credit does not carry over.

When the credit shows up

The price is not reduced at booking. You pay in full and the credit arrives later. Sources disagree on how long that takes:

Source Stated timing
Chase's terms Within 3 business days, up to 4 weeks
One Mile at a Time Within two days of the purchase posting
Upgraded Points 1 to 2 billing cycles

Chase can take the credit back if the booking is canceled, returned or changed, or if you close the account within 90 days of receiving it.

The costs that do not show on the statement

  • No points on the credited part. The first $100 of Chase Travel hotel purchases each year earns no points. The rest of the booking earns 5x.
  • Hotel loyalty. Chase Travel is a third-party booking channel. At the large chains, such bookings generally earn no hotel points and no elite benefits. The Points Guy notes some listings carry a loyalty tag, but points are not guaranteed.
  • Price. Rates in Chase Travel can be higher than the hotel's own rate or a member rate. A $100 credit on a stay that costs $40 more than booking direct is worth $60.

A simple check: price the same room and dates on the hotel's site. Subtract any difference from $100. That is what the credit is worth to you on that stay.

The sum against the fee

Used in full on a stay you would have booked anyway at the same price, the credit is $100 against a $95 fee. Used on a pricier rate, or on a stay you would not have taken, it is worth less. How this fits with the rest of the card's first year is on the first-year plan page.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone with hotel elite status who would lose upgrades, breakfast or night credits by booking through a third party.
  • Anyone who rarely pays for hotels, or who stays in vacation rentals booked direct.
  • Anyone who finds the same room cheaper elsewhere by close to $100.
  • Anyone who needs a pay-at-hotel rate. The credit is described as needing a prepaid booking.

Quick answers

Is the Sapphire Preferred hotel credit $50 or $100? It is up to $100 per account anniversary year. Chase raised it from $50 on June 15, 2026.

Does a hotel booked directly with the hotel count? No. Only hotel stays bought through Chase Travel with the card count.

Does the credit roll over? No. It resets each account anniversary year and unused credit is lost.
