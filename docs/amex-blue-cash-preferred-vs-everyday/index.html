Blue Cash Preferred or Blue Cash Everyday: at what grocery spend does the annual fee pay off?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Blue Cash Preferred's $95 annual fee pays off against the no-fee Blue Cash Everyday once you spend more than about $3,167 a year at US supermarkets, which is roughly $264 a month. The most the grocery rate can put you ahead is $85 a year, because the 6% rate stops at $6,000 of grocery spending.

The break-even sum

On groceries, the Preferred earns 6% and the Everyday earns 3%. The difference is 3 cents on each dollar.

$95 fee ÷ 0.03 = $3,166.67 a year

$3,166.67 ÷ 12 = $263.89 a month

Below that amount, the Everyday leaves you with more money. Above it, the Preferred does, up to the cap.

Past $6,000 both cards earn 1% on groceries, so the gap stops growing.

NerdWallet puts the monthly break-even at $267, slightly above the $264 worked out here. The difference is small and may come from rounding.

In the first year the Preferred has no annual fee, so it is ahead at any grocery spend for those 12 months.

Things that move the break-even

Streaming. The Preferred earns 6% on select US streaming and has a Disney streaming credit that is $36 a year larger. If you already pay for Disney+, Hulu or ESPN and would use the full credit on either card, the fee gap shrinks to $59, and the grocery break-even falls to about $1,967 a year ($59 ÷ 0.03).

Transit. The Preferred earns 3% on transit, the Everyday 1%. Each $1,000 of commuting costs adds $20 to the Preferred's side.

Online shopping. This goes the other way. The Everyday earns 3% at US online retailers on up to $6,000 a year, and the Preferred earns 1%. Each $1,000 of online shopping is worth $20 more on the Everyday. A household that spends $3,000 a year online gives up $60 by choosing the Preferred, which pushes the grocery break-even up to about $5,167 a year (($95 + $60) ÷ 0.03).

Gas. Both earn 3%. The Everyday's rate stops at $6,000 a year; the Preferred's has no stated cap.

What counts as a US supermarket

Amex's supermarket category covers traditional grocery stores. One Mile at a Time notes that superstores such as Walmart and Target, warehouse clubs such as Costco and Sam's Club, and convenience stores generally do not count. Purchases there earn 1% on either card.

If most of your food shopping is at a superstore or warehouse club, the sums above do not apply to that spending, and the fee will not pay off through groceries.

Who should skip the Preferred

  • Households that spend under about $264 a month at traditional supermarkets and do not use streaming or transit much.
  • People who buy groceries mainly at Walmart, Target or Costco.
  • People who do more shopping online than in grocery stores.
  • People who want a card for travel abroad. Both cards charge a 2.7% foreign transaction fee.

People who want points for travel, not cash back, can compare the Gold Card's grocery sums.

Welcome offers on both cards vary by applicant and change, and are not part of the sums above.

Quick answers

What is the break-even grocery spend? About $3,167 a year, or $264 a month, at US supermarkets. That is the $95 fee divided by the 3-point difference in cash back.

What is the most the Preferred can earn over the Everyday on groceries? $85 a year after the fee. The 6% rate applies to the first $6,000 of supermarket spending each year.

Does Costco count as a supermarket for the 6%? No. Warehouse clubs and superstores generally are not counted as US supermarkets.
