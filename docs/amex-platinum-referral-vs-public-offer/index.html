Is the Amex Platinum referral offer higher or lower than the public offer?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Neither route is reliably higher. In November 2026 the public page and referral links both lead to the same kind of offer, worded as high as 175,000 points, and Amex shows each applicant their own number before they accept. The only way to compare is to look at the number each route gives you.

The Points Guy reports that the spending requirement and time frame are generally the same across offers. The points figure is the part that varies. Upgraded Points calls the top figure highly targeted and says many applicants are shown less.

What sources say about referral against public

The sources do not fully agree.

  • AwardWallet (June 2026) says that most of the time a referral offer matches the public one, that sometimes it is not as good, and that rare referral-only offers have been higher.
  • Upgraded Points (October 2026) says it is "not uncommon" for a referral offer to be higher than the public offer. It does not mention lower ones.
  • The Points Guy notes that referral offers can differ a lot from one person's link to another's, even for the same card.

Both older offers have expired. They show that the gap can open in either direction and close again. The spending requirement also moved: it rose from $8,000 to $12,000 on February 5, 2026.

How to compare the two

  1. Open the public Platinum page and go through the application until Amex shows your offer. Write down the points and the spending requirement.
  2. Do the same through a referral link.
  3. Compare the two numbers. If they match, the route makes no difference to you.
  4. Accept only the one you want. Declining an offer has no effect on your credit score, according to One Mile at a Time, which reports there is no hard credit check unless you accept.

Read each screen with care. Some reader comments at One Mile at a Time claim an offer was shown only after a hard credit check. That is not verified.

What does not change with the route

  • The lifetime rule. If you have or had the Platinum Card, you may get no welcome offer by either route. See the once-per-lifetime rule. Whether referral applications are treated any differently is not confirmed; no source read says they are.
  • The annual fee. It is $895 either way.
  • The spending requirement. A higher points figure is worth little if you cannot reach $12,000 in six months with your normal spending. See what counts toward it.

A person who shares a referral link can earn a bonus from Amex if you are approved. That is a reason for them to prefer their link. It is not a reason for you to skip the comparison. How referral links work covers this.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone who has held the Platinum Card before and is applying mainly for the welcome offer.
  • Anyone who would need to spend more than usual to reach $12,000 in six months. Extra purchases or interest can cost more than the points are worth.
  • Anyone who would not use enough of the card's credits to cover $895 a year once the first year is over. See what the credits are worth.
  • Anyone shown a low offer by both routes who is in no hurry. Offers change, and you can decline.

No source can tell you what your offer will be. Offers vary by applicant and change without notice.

Quick answers

Is a referral offer always higher than the public offer? No. Sources report referral offers that were higher, lower and identical. In November 2026 both routes use the same wording.

Does checking both routes hurt my credit score? Reviewers report that seeing your offer uses a soft check, and a hard inquiry comes only if you accept the card.

Is the spending requirement different through a referral link? Usually not. Both routes currently state $12,000 in the first six months, but read the terms on your own offer screen.
