Chase Sapphire Reserve or Citi Strata Elite: which premium card costs less to keep?

Facts checked November 21, 20265 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

On the fee alone the Citi Strata Elite costs less, at $595 a year against $795, but the Sapphire Reserve's $300 credit works on any travel and brings it to $495. The Strata Elite gets to $295 only with a two-night hotel stay booked through Citi Travel, so it is the cheaper card to keep for readers who would make that booking anyway.

The two issuers print different Priority Pass lounge counts, 1,300+ and 1,500+. The Splurge credit brands are 1stDibs, American Airlines, Best Buy, Future Personal Training and Live Nation.

The Strata Elite sum

  • Fee: $595.
  • Hotel benefit: $300, taken off the price when a two-night stay is booked through Citi Travel. Leaves $295.
  • Splurge credit: $200, for example on American Airlines or Best Buy purchases. Leaves $95.
  • Blacklane: $200 in two halves. Leaves minus $105, but only for a reader who would hire a chauffeured car twice a year.

Citigold clients get a further $145 credit each year, which brings the starting fee to $450.

A reader who uses the hotel benefit and the Splurge credit, and ignores Blacklane, keeps the card for $95 a year.

The Reserve sum

  • Fee: $795.
  • Travel credit: $300, applied to any travel charge. Leaves $495.
  • The Edit: $250 per prepaid two-night stay, up to two a year. One stay leaves $245; two leave minus $5.
  • Dining: $150 in each half of the year at Exclusive Tables restaurants on OpenTable. Used in full with one Edit stay, it leaves minus $55.

A reader who uses only the travel credit keeps the card for $495 a year. The page on what the Reserve credits are worth goes through each one.

Earning also differs:

  • Flights and hotels booked direct: Reserve, at 4x against 1.5x. On $8,000 a year that is 32,000 points against 12,000.
  • Everyday spending outside travel and dining: Strata Elite, at 1.5x against 1x. On $20,000 that is 30,000 points against 20,000.
  • Flies American: Strata Elite. Of these two, only Citi moves points to American AAdvantage, and the card adds four Admirals Club passes.
  • Flies United or Southwest, or stays at Hyatt: Reserve. None of the three is a Citi partner in the lists read.
  • Uses lounges at Boston, Dallas Fort Worth, JFK, LaGuardia, Las Vegas, Philadelphia, Phoenix or San Diego: Reserve, for its own lounges. Citi has none. See Reserve lounge access.

Trip delay, trip cancellation and rental car cover amounts for the Strata Elite were not confirmed in the pages read, so travel insurance is left out of this comparison.

Who should skip both

  • Anyone who carries a balance. Interest costs more than either card returns.
  • Anyone who travels once or twice a year. The $95 Sapphire Preferred or Citi Strata Premier costs less.
  • For the Strata Elite: anyone who books hotels direct for loyalty benefits and would not use Citi Travel.
  • For the Reserve: anyone who would use only the travel credit and rarely sees a lounge. $495 a year is the cost.

The Venture X comparison covers a third premium card at $395. Welcome offers on both cards vary by applicant and change, so they are left out of the sums.

Quick answers

Which card has the lower annual fee? The Citi Strata Elite, at $595 against $795 for the Sapphire Reserve.

Does the Strata Elite have a general travel credit like the Reserve's $300? No. Its $300 hotel benefit applies only to one stay of two nights or more booked through Citi Travel.

Do Citi points transfer to American Airlines? Yes, at 1:1 from the Strata Elite. Chase points do not transfer to American.
