# What credit score do you need for the Chase Sapphire Preferred?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/chase-sapphire-preferred-credit-score-needed/
Facts checked: 2026-11-22
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Chase does not publish a minimum credit score for the Sapphire Preferred. Sources that track approvals report a FICO score of about 670 as the low end and 700 or higher as the usual range, and Chase also weighs income, recent card openings and the credit it has already given you.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## What Chase says

Chase's card page gives no score, no income figure and no minimum credit line. It says only that the card is subject to credit approval. The Points Guy states it directly:

> "Chase doesn't publish a minimum credit score requirement." (The Points Guy)

Every number below is a report from a named source, not a Chase rule and not a promise of approval.

## What named sources report

| Source | Figure reported | Date of article |
|---|---|---|
| The Points Guy | FICO of about 700 or higher | July 2026 |
| CNBC Select | At least 670; above 700 gives better odds | October 2026 |
| Upgraded Points | Wait for at least 670; most cardholders at 720 or higher | July 2025 |
| Motley Fool Money | Most approved applicants above 700; 680 to 699 approved less often | August 2025 |
| FinanceBuzz | Good to excellent credit, with good starting at 670 | November 2026 |

The sources differ on where the line sits, 670 or 700, and none claims a cutoff. Upgraded Points notes approvals with scores in the 600s and denials with scores in the 800s. A score in the reported range does not mean approval.

## What else Chase looks at

- **Cards opened in the past 24 months.** Chase generally declines applicants who opened five or more personal cards at any bank in that time, whatever their score. This is unofficial; see [how the 5/24 rule counts cards](/chase-5-24-rule-explained/).
- **Income.** Chase states no minimum. Upgraded Points suggests at least $30,000 a year, its own view.
- **Debt against income.** Motley Fool Money says a low ratio helps.
- **Length of credit history.** Upgraded Points and Motley Fool Money both suggest two years or more.
- **Credit Chase already gives you.** The Points Guy says Chase may limit the total credit it extends across your cards.
- **Recent applications.** CNBC Select lists them as a factor. Each application also adds a hard inquiry; see [what an application does to a score](/does-applying-for-a-travel-card-hurt-credit-score/).
- **Whether you already hold the card.** Approval and the welcome offer are separate questions. The offer has [its own eligibility rules](/chase-sapphire-bonus-eligibility-2026/), and offers vary by applicant and change.

## The Visa Signature credit line

FinanceBuzz and Upgraded Points both report that an approved Sapphire Preferred comes with a credit limit of at least $5,000, and FinanceBuzz ties that to the card being a Visa Signature. Chase's public card page does not state this figure, so it is reported, not confirmed by Chase.

If the report holds, Chase has to be willing to extend $5,000, which depends on income and on your existing Chase credit lines.

## Checking before you apply

Chase has a page that checks for preapproved offers. You enter basic details such as name and address. Chase says filling out the form does not affect your credit score, while an application does. The page does not say an offer guarantees approval.

## If Chase says no

A denial comes with a notice giving the reasons. The Points Guy and AwardWallet both describe calling Chase's reconsideration line to ask for a second look.

| Point | What is reported |
|---|---|
| Result | Not guaranteed, per both sources |
| Too much total credit | An agent may move or lower credit on an existing Chase card (The Points Guy, AwardWallet) |
| 5/24 | Agents generally cannot override it (The Points Guy, AwardWallet) |
| Phone number | AwardWallet lists 888-270-2127 for personal cards; another listing differs, so check your denial notice |

## Who should skip it for now

- Anyone at or over 5/24. A high score does not get around it.
- Anyone with a FICO score well under 670. Every source read puts the reported range above that.
- Anyone with a credit history shorter than about two years, going by the two sources that give a figure.
- Anyone who would carry a balance. Interest outweighs points.

## Quick answers

**Is there a minimum credit score for the Sapphire Preferred?**
Chase publishes none. Named sources report roughly 670 as the low end and 700 or more as typical.

**Does a score above 700 mean approval?**
No. Chase also looks at recent card openings, income and existing Chase credit, and denials with high scores are reported.

**Does checking for a preapproved offer affect my credit score?**
Chase says filling out its form does not. Submitting an application does.
