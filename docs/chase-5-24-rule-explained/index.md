# What is Chase's 5/24 rule, and how do you count your cards?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/chase-5-24-rule-explained/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

The 5/24 rule is an unofficial Chase practice: if you have opened five or more personal credit cards, from any bank, in the past 24 months, Chase will generally decline you for most of its cards. To count, list every card on your credit report by its opening date and count the ones from the last 24 months, closed cards included.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## Where the rule comes from

Chase has never published it. The rule is pieced together from applicants' reported results, and guides such as The Points Guy's treat it as well established. Because it is unofficial, the details below are reported patterns, not Chase policy in writing.

It applies to most Chase cards, including both Sapphire cards, the Freedom cards, the Ink business cards, and Chase's airline and hotel cards.

## What counts and what does not

| Account | Counts toward 5/24? |
|---|---|
| Personal credit cards from any bank, opened in the past 24 months | Yes |
| Cards you opened and then closed in that time | Yes |
| Authorized user cards that show on your credit report | Yes, but see below |
| Store cards on a national network such as Visa or Mastercard | Yes |
| Store cards usable at one store only | Some reports say yes |
| Business cards from Discover and TD Bank | Yes |
| Most Capital One business cards | Yes (reported exceptions: Venture X Business, Spark Cash Plus) |
| Most other business cards, including Chase's own | No |
| Denied applications and credit inquiries | No |
| Auto loans, student loans, mortgages | No |
| Changing one card to another with the same bank | Usually no, unless it creates a new account |
| Charge cards | Not confirmed in the sources read |

The Points Guy's rule of thumb: if it is a credit card and it appears on your personal credit report, assume it counts.

One point that trips people up: Chase business cards do not add to your count, but you generally still need to be under 5/24 to be approved for one.

## Authorized user cards

If someone added you to their card in the past 24 months, it normally appears on your report, and Chase's system counts it. That can make you look over the limit when your own cards are under it.

Two things are reported to help:

- After a denial, call Chase's reconsideration line and point out which accounts are authorized user accounts. An agent may set them aside. This is manual and may not work.
- Ask to be removed as an authorized user at least a month before you apply, so the report has time to update.

## How to count your cards

1. Get your credit report. The Points Guy's guide suggests Experian's free report; be careful not to sign up for a paid plan by mistake.
2. Sort the accounts by date opened.
3. Count every credit card opened in the past 24 months. Include closed cards and authorized user cards. Leave out loans.
4. Add any Discover, TD Bank or Capital One business cards if they show on the report.
5. Four or fewer means you are under 5/24. Five or more means you are at or over it.

## When a card stops counting

A card drops off on the first day of the 25th month after it was opened, not on the exact anniversary. The Points Guy's example: if your fifth most recent card was opened in October 2024, the earliest day to apply is November 1, 2026.

| Fifth card opened | Earliest date under 5/24 |
|---|---|
| October 2024 | November 1, 2026 |
| January 2025 | February 1, 2027 |
| June 2025 | July 1, 2027 |

## Other limits reported alongside it

- Some applicants report Chase will not approve more than two new accounts within 30 days.
- Being under 5/24 does not mean approval. Credit score, income and existing debt still matter.
- Being under 5/24 does not make you eligible for a welcome bonus. The Sapphire cards have [separate bonus rules](/chase-sapphire-bonus-eligibility-2026/).

## Who should skip worrying about it

- Anyone who has opened one or two cards in two years. You are well under.
- Anyone who is far over 5/24 and has no wish to stop opening other cards for a long stretch. Other banks do not use this rule.
- Anyone thinking of opening cards only to collect bonuses. Each new account affects your credit, and bonuses change and vary by applicant.

## Quick answers

**Do business cards count toward 5/24?**
Most do not, including Chase's own. Business cards from Discover, TD Bank and most from Capital One are reported to count.

**Does a closed card still count?**
Yes. Chase looks at when the account was opened, not whether it is still open.

**Does a denied application count?**
No. Only opened accounts count, not inquiries.
