Is the Amex Gold Card worth its annual fee if you cook at home most nights?

Facts checked November 20, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

For a home cook the Amex Gold Card can cover its $325 annual fee, but only if you spend a lot at US supermarkets or you would use its monthly credits anyway. On grocery points alone, valued at 1 cent each, it takes about $8,125 of supermarket spending a year to pay the fee back.

What the card costs and earns

The annual fee is $325. The earning rates that matter in a kitchen:

  • 4 points per dollar at US supermarkets, on up to $25,000 per calendar year, then 1 point.
  • 4 points per dollar at restaurants worldwide, on up to $50,000 per calendar year, then 1 point.
  • 3 points on flights booked with airlines or through Amex Travel, and 1 point on most other purchases.

One limit matters a lot for groceries: superstores and warehouse clubs are not counted as supermarkets. Shopping at Walmart, Target, Costco or Sam's Club earns 1 point, not 4.

The grocery sum

What 4 points per dollar is worth depends on how you use the points. Covering card charges gives about 0.6 cents per point. Flights through Amex Travel give 1 cent. The Points Guy values a point at 2 cents when moved to airline partners; One Mile at a Time says 1.7 cents. See what a point is worth by use.

Grocery spend needed to match the $325 fee with no credits used:

  • At 0.6 cents per point: about $13,542 a year.
  • At 1 cent per point: $8,125 a year, about $677 a month.
  • At 2 cents per point: about $4,063 a year, about $339 a month.

The credits, from a home cook's side

The card has four recurring credits. All need enrollment, and unused amounts are lost each month or half-year.

Credit Amount Where Useful if you cook at home?
Dining Up to $10 a month ($120 a year) Grubhub, Buffalo Wild Wings, Five Guys, The Cheesecake Factory, Wonder Only if you order from one of these each month
Uber Cash Up to $10 a month ($120 a year) US Uber rides and Uber Eats Only if you ride or order monthly

Dunkin' Up to $7 a month ($84 a year) US Dunkin' locations Only if you already buy there

The four add up to $424 a year on paper. A person who rarely eats out will use much less. An example: if you use the Uber Cash and the Dunkin' credit in full ($204) and nothing else, the fee you still have to earn back is $121. At 1 cent per point that takes about $3,025 of supermarket spending a year, roughly $252 a month.

A credit only counts as money saved if you would have bought the thing anyway.

Compared with a cash back grocery card

The Blue Cash Preferred, also from Amex, pays 6% cash back at US supermarkets on up to $6,000 a year, for a $95 fee after the first year. On $6,000 of groceries that is $360 in cash. The Gold Card earns 24,000 points on the same spending, worth $144 to $480 depending on use. The Blue Cash break-even sums are here.

The Gold Card comes out ahead for a home cook mainly when grocery spending is above $6,000 a year, when points go to airline partners, or when the credits fit existing habits.

Who should skip it

  • People who buy most groceries at Walmart, Target, Costco or another superstore or warehouse club.
  • People who spend under about $340 a month at supermarkets and would not use the credits.
  • People who want cash back. Used against card charges, points are worth about 0.6 cents each.
  • People who will not track four separate credits with monthly or half-year deadlines.
  • People who want airport lounge access. This card has none.

Welcome offers on this card vary by applicant and change, and people who have had the card before may not get one. See the once per lifetime rule.

Quick answers

Does Costco or Walmart earn 4 points on the Gold Card? No. Superstores and warehouse clubs are not counted as US supermarkets, so they earn 1 point per dollar.

How much grocery spending covers the $325 fee? About $8,125 a year if a point is worth 1 cent to you and you use none of the credits. Less if you use the credits or transfer points to airlines.

Is there a cap on the 4 points at supermarkets? Yes. The 4 points apply to the first $25,000 of US supermarket purchases per calendar year, then 1 point.
