# When should you pay with points, and when with cash?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/points-or-cash-when-to-pay-with-points/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

Work out cents per point: take the cash price, subtract the taxes and fees you still pay on the award, and divide by the points needed. If the answer is below what your points fetch the simple way (1 cent for Chase, 0.6 to 1 cent for Amex), pay cash. If it is above the 1.7 to 2 cents that published valuations use, points are doing well.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The check, in three steps

1. Find the cash price of the exact flight or room, on the same dates.
2. Find the points price, and the taxes, fees and surcharges charged with it.
3. Cents per point = (cash price minus award taxes and fees) divided by points, times 100.

Use a cash price you would in fact pay. A $6,000 business-class fare you would never buy does not make your points worth 6 cents each to you.

## What to compare the answer with

| Yardstick | Amex points | Chase points | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| The floor: cash or statement credit | 0.6 cents | 1 cent | The Points Guy, CNBC Select |
| Flights through the card's travel site | 1 cent | 1 cent; up to 2 cents with a Points Boost on a Sapphire Reserve | The Points Guy, Chase |
| Published average through transfer partners | 2.0 cents (The Points Guy); 1.7 cents (One Mile at a Time) | 2.05 cents (The Points Guy); 1.7 cents (One Mile at a Time) | November 2026 valuations |

Reading the result:

- **Below the floor.** Pay cash. The points would buy more as a plain credit or travel-site booking.
- **Between the floor and about 1.7 cents.** A fair use, not a strong one. Paying with points makes sense if you have more points than plans for them.
- **About 1.7 to 2 cents or more.** In line with or above what experienced users average.

The floors come from [what an Amex point is worth](/amex-membership-rewards-points-value/) and [Chase's Points Boost](/chase-points-boost-explained/).

## Three worked examples

The award prices are from the sources named. The cash fares are illustrations, not quotes.

| Booking | Points and fees (source) | Cash fare (illustration) | Cents per point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flying Blue business class, US to Europe, one way | 60,000 miles plus $608 (Flying Blue's example, via The Points Guy) | $2,400 | (2,400 − 608) ÷ 60,000 = 2.99 |
| The same seat during a fare sale | 60,000 miles plus $608 | $1,300 | (1,300 − 608) ÷ 60,000 = 1.15 |
| 50,000 Amex points moved to Hilton (100,000 Hilton points) | No fees | $400 of hotel nights, at The Points Guy's 0.4 cents per Hilton point | 400 ÷ 50,000 = 0.8 |

The first is a strong use. The second barely beats booking through the travel site. The third is below the 1 cent that Amex Travel gives on flights, so those points would go further on airfare.

## Three things the sum leaves out

**Points you do not earn.** A cash ticket earns points; an award ticket does not. The Platinum Card earns 5 points per dollar on flights booked with airlines or Amex Travel. On a $1,000 fare that is 5,000 points, worth $50 to $100 at 1 to 2 cents each. Award tickets also usually earn no airline miles.

**Taxes and surcharges.** They vary widely. The Points Guy puts a Virgin Atlantic business-class award from New York to London at 29,000 points plus about $700 one way. Leave that out and the sum flatters the award.

**Flexibility.** A transfer to an airline cannot be reversed, and the miles then follow that airline's rules. Flying Blue's lowest award fares, sold since September 8, 2026, allow no changes or refunds.

## When cash is usually right

- Low fares. As an illustration, a $250 domestic ticket priced at 25,000 miles is 1 cent per point before fees.
- Hotel transfers other than Hyatt. The Points Guy values Marriott at 0.75 cents, IHG at 0.6, Hilton at 0.4 and Wyndham at 0.55 per point.
- Gift cards and checkout purchases with Amex points, at 0.5 to 1 cent.
- Any trip where the award surcharge is close to the cash fare.

## When points are usually right

- Long-haul business class with an open award seat. See the prices for [Europe](/how-many-points-for-a-flight-to-europe/) and [Japan](/how-many-points-for-a-flight-to-japan/).
- Economy on dates when cash fares are high but the award price is fixed.
- Hyatt nights from a Sapphire Reserve at 1 to 1; The Points Guy values a Hyatt point at 1.65 cents.

Which partners clear the bar most often: [Chase partners](/chase-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/) and [Amex partners](/amex-transfer-partners-which-are-worth-it/).

## Who should skip the math

- People with a cash-back card. There is nothing to compare.
- People who hold few points and have no trip planned. Points do not earn interest, and programs can raise award prices without notice.
- People who value simplicity over the last half cent. Booking through the travel site at 1 cent takes no award search.

## Quick answers

**What is a good cents-per-point value?**
Above about 1.7 to 2 cents matches the published averages for Amex and Chase points. Above the 1 cent travel-site rate is acceptable.

**Should taxes be subtracted first?**
Yes. Subtract the taxes and fees charged on the award from the cash price before dividing by the points.

**Is it wrong to use points at 1 cent?**
No. It is the base travel-site rate for both programs. It is simply not more than the simple option gives.
