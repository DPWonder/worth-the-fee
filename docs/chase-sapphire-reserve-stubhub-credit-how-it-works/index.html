How does the Chase Sapphire Reserve StubHub credit work, and what counts?

Facts checked November 22, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

The Sapphire Reserve pays back up to $150 from January to June and up to $150 from July to December on purchases made directly with StubHub or viagogo, through December 31, 2027. You activate it once in your Chase account before buying, and whatever you do not use in a half-year is gone.

Activation

Chase's terms call for a one-time activation. Chase's own guide says purchases made before activation do not qualify. AwardWallet describes the path as Benefits and travel, then Benefits, then the StubHub credit tile.

Doctor of Credit mentions reader reports of a credit after late activation. That conflicts with Chase's guide.

What counts

  • Tickets bought on stubhub.com, viagogo.com or either app, paid with the card. Chase's guide names sports, concerts and theater.
  • viagogo counts the same way as StubHub and shares the same $150 limit.
  • Parking passes sold on StubHub: not confirmed. Chase's terms do not mention them.

What does not count

  • Gift cards. Chase's terms exclude them.
  • Events in the United Kingdom. Chase's terms say these are sent to stubhub.co.uk, a separate company, and do not qualify.
  • A purchase that is later returned, canceled or changed. Chase can take the credit back, and can also do so if the account closes within 90 days of a credit.

Do fees and taxes count?

Chase's terms do not say. Doctor of Credit reports that they do, with the example of a $130 ticket that comes to $151 after fees and receives the full $150. AwardWallet's examples also use order totals. Treat this as reported, not as written policy.

When the credit posts

Source Stated timing
Chase's terms Within 3 business days, up to 4 weeks
Chase's guide One to two billing cycles
The Points Guy Reports of about a day
Doctor of Credit Most within a few days

Each period ends at 11:59 PM Eastern on its last day. The Points Guy reports a June 30 purchase that posted July 1 and was counted in the second half.

Thrifty Traveler says the purchase earns the card's usual 1 point per dollar.

The sums for one ticket purchase a year

These are resale sites, so a ticket can cost more than its face price, and fees are added.

Reader Yearly value
Buys no resale tickets $0
One purchase a year, $150 or more $150
One purchase a year, $90 $90
One purchase in each half, $150 or more each time $300
Buys only to use the credit, $200 each half $300 back on $400 of new spending

Someone who buys once a year gets at most half the listed $300. The last row is a cost of $100, not a gain of $300.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone who does not go to ticketed events. This credit is then worth $0 toward the $795 fee.
  • Anyone who buys from the venue or team at face price and would pay more on a resale site.
  • Anyone going to events in the United Kingdom only.
  • Anyone who will not track two deadlines a year.

For a credit on the same half-year schedule, see how the dining credit works.

Quick answers

Does viagogo count as well as StubHub? Yes. Chase's terms cover purchases made directly with either one, under the same $150 limit.

Does unused credit from January to June carry into July? No. Each half-year's $150 expires at the end of its period.

Do StubHub gift cards trigger the credit? No. Chase's terms exclude gift cards.
