Rakuten app or browser extension: which tracks purchases more reliably?

The short answer

No source gives tracking figures for one against the other, so a measured answer is not confirmed. Rakuten's own guidance is to use the extension on a computer and the app on a phone, and each one fails in different, known situations.

A Rakuten blog post from January 30, 2026 calls the extension on desktop plus the app on mobile the "most reliable setup". That is Rakuten's recommendation, not a test result.

Both do the same job: each starts a shopping trip, Rakuten's record that you reached the store through it. No trip, no cash back.

When the extension fails

  • You did not activate. Rakuten's help article says the extension notifies you and you activate, on every visit. A second order at the same store needs a new activation. (Rakuten's blog says the extension activates automatically; its help article says otherwise. The safer habit is to look for the confirmation.)
  • Another extension took the credit. A click from a different coupon or rewards extension after activating turns Rakuten's cash back off.
  • A blocker is running. Ad blockers, VPNs, cookie-blocking security software and private or incognito windows can stop activation.
  • Safari at some stores. Rakuten says Safari's tracking prevention can block cash back at stores that have not adjusted their sites. Its advice on a computer is to switch to Chrome.
  • The store is not offered through the extension. If no activate button appears, the store may not be available that way.
  • Items were in the cart first. Some stores will not link an order if items were added before activating.

The toolbar icon tells you where you stand on a computer:

Icon color Meaning
Purple The store offers no cash back
Red Cash back is available but not activated
Blue Cash back is activated

When the app fails

  • You leave the window the app opened. Rakuten says the window you buy in must have come from a Rakuten link. Finishing the order in a different browser tab breaks that.
  • The store's own app. What happens if the order is completed in the retailer's own app is not covered in the Rakuten articles read. It is not confirmed, so the cautious path is to finish checkout in the page the Rakuten app opened.
  • A coupon from elsewhere. Codes not listed on Rakuten may void cash back, in the app as on a computer.

The iPhone middle path

On an iPhone there is a third option: the Safari extension, which comes inside the Rakuten iOS app. It needs the latest app and the latest iOS Safari, has to be switched on under Manage Extensions, and needs "Always Allow" on every website.

It fails if content blockers are on. Rakuten's fix is to shop in the app instead, or to turn content blockers off in Settings while shopping.

Which to use where

Situation What Rakuten's pages point to
Shopping on a computer Extension, in Chrome if Safari is blocked at that store
Android phone App (no extension exists)
iPhone with content blockers App
iPhone, shopping in Safari Safari extension, with blockers off
Restaurants and in-store offers App only

Whichever you use, the check is the same: look for the activation confirmation before you pay, then look for a pending amount within a day or two. If it is missing, see what to do about missing cash back.

Who should skip it

  • Skip the extension if you do not want a tool that reads the pages you visit. It collects browsing data whether or not you interact with it; see extension privacy. You can start from Rakuten's website instead.
  • Skip the app if you do all your shopping on a computer and never use in-store or restaurant offers.

Quick answers

Is the Rakuten app more reliable than the extension? Not confirmed. No published figures compare them. Rakuten suggests the extension on a computer and the app on a phone.

Is there a Rakuten extension for Android? No. Rakuten's help pages say Android users should use the app.

How do you know cash back is active? On a computer the toolbar icon turns blue. In any case, look for the activation confirmation before paying.
