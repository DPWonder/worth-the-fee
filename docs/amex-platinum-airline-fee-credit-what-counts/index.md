# What does the Amex Platinum $200 airline fee credit actually cover?

Source: https://itsworththefee.com/amex-platinum-airline-fee-credit-what-counts/
Facts checked: 2026-11-20
Publisher: Worth the Fee (independent; some pages end with a disclosed referral link)

It covers incidental fees, such as checked bags, seat selection and in-flight food, on one airline you choose in advance, up to $200 per calendar year. It does not cover airline tickets, award tickets, upgrades, miles or gift cards.

This page is information, not financial advice.

## The rules in short

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Amount | Up to $200 per calendar year, January 1 to December 31 |
| Rollover | None |
| Airline | One, chosen in your Amex account before you pay the fee |
| Changing airline | Once a year, in January. Last year's choice carries over if you do nothing |
| How it pays | A statement credit, within 8 weeks under the terms; often sooner |
| Additional cards | Their fees on the same airline count toward the one $200 |

You get back only what you spend on eligible fees. Someone who pays $70 in bag fees in a year gets $70.

The six airlines both sources list are safe to assume. Amex says the list can change, so the selection screen in your own account is the final word.

## What counts

The Points Guy and AwardWallet list these as generally eligible, when the airline charges them separately from the ticket:

- Checked bag fees, including overweight and oversized bags
- Seat assignment fees
- Change fees
- In-flight food and drinks
- In-flight entertainment charged by the airline
- Pet fees
- Phone booking fees
- Airport lounge day passes and annual lounge memberships

## What does not count

- Airline tickets
- Award tickets
- Upgrades
- Buying or transferring miles
- Gift cards
- Duty-free purchases
- In-flight Wi-Fi billed by an outside provider
- Fees charged by a partner airline, even on a trip booked through your chosen airline
- Fees charged before you selected the airline
- A seat fee paid in the same charge as the ticket

On taxes and fees for award tickets, sources disagree. One Mile at a Time lists award ticket fees as often refunded. AwardWallet lists them as not counting, and The Points Guy calls them unofficial and unreliable. Do not plan around them.

## Why a fee that should count sometimes does not

Amex does not review each charge by hand. It relies on how the airline labels the transaction. If a fee is labeled as airfare, no credit arrives. The Points Guy notes that United lounge passes may be labeled this way. If a fee you believe qualifies has not been credited after 8 weeks, the terms say to call the number on the back of the card.

## Older workarounds

Some cardholders once used the credit on United TravelBank funds or small Delta gift cards. Upgraded Points reports that these stopped working in early 2026. That is one source, so it is not confirmed.

## What it is worth to you

Work it out from fees you already pay on one airline in a year.

| Your flying | Likely yearly value |
|---|---|
| A family checking bags on the same airline several times a year | Up to $200 |
| Two round trips, one checked bag each way | Four bag fees at your airline's price |
| Carry-on only, no paid seats | $0 |
| Free bags already, through airline status or an airline card | Little, unless you pay for seats or lounge passes |

Bag prices vary by airline and route; check your airline's current fee.

## Who should skip it

Leave this credit out of your sums on the $895 fee if:

- You travel with a carry-on and take whatever seat is assigned.
- You already get free checked bags on the airline you fly most.
- You mostly fly airlines that are not on the list, including all foreign airlines.
- You expected it to reduce the price of a ticket. It will not.

For the other credits, see [what the credits are worth](/amex-platinum-credits-real-value/). For light travelers, see [the occasional flyer page](/amex-platinum-worth-it-occasional-flyer/).

## Quick answers

**Does the Amex Platinum airline credit cover plane tickets?**
No. Tickets, award tickets and upgrades are excluded. It covers extra fees such as checked bags.

**Can you change your selected airline during the year?**
The rule is once a year, in January. The Points Guy reports Amex has sometimes allowed a later change, but that is not guaranteed.

**Does the unused part carry over to next year?**
No. The credit resets on January 1 and anything unused on December 31 is lost.
