What counts toward the Chase Sapphire Reserve's $300 travel credit, and when does it reset?

Facts checked November 21, 20264 min readHow we check facts

This page contains a referral link. We earn points or cash if you sign up through it. How we earn.

The short answer

Any purchase that Chase codes as travel counts, including airfare, hotels, car rentals, trains, taxis, tolls and parking, with no sign-up and no booking site required. The credit resets each account anniversary year, not on January 1, and the part of a purchase it covers earns no points.

What counts

Chase's terms say the credit applies to "purchases made in the travel category". Chase's category list names these merchants:

  • Airlines
  • Hotels, motels and timeshares
  • Car rental agencies
  • Cruise lines
  • Travel agencies and discount travel sites
  • Campgrounds
  • Passenger trains, buses, taxis, limousines and ferries
  • Toll bridges and highways
  • Parking lots and garages

Chase's guide gives taxi fares and highway tolls as examples, and The Points Guy says everyday parking can qualify. One Mile at a Time (June 2, 2026) says rideshare and subway fares count too. Rideshare is not named on Chase's own list.

What does not count

Chase's category list leaves out sightseeing, excursions and tourist attractions; in-flight goods and services; purchases on board a cruise ship; merchants inside hotels and airports; RV and boat rentals; public campgrounds; rentals for hauling; and gift card merchants or merchants that sell points or miles. The Points Guy adds theme park tickets bought from the park and ski lift tickets.

The merchant's code decides, not what you bought. Chase's terms say it does not determine whether a merchant bills a purchase under the right category.

How it is applied

The credit posts by itself as a statement credit. It can be spread over many purchases or used on one.

Purchases Credit
$270 airfare, then $180 hotel $270, then $30
One $500 car rental $300

Both examples are Chase's.

When it resets

The credit runs on the account anniversary year, not the calendar year. Chase's terms define the first year as the account open date through the first statement date after the anniversary. After that, each year is the next 12 monthly billing cycles.

For an account opened March 10, 2026, the first credit year ends on the first statement date after March 10, 2027.

The Points Guy reports one exception: accounts opened before May 21, 2017 get the credit by calendar year, starting after the December statement closes. That is not confirmed on a Chase page read for this article.

Do the purchases earn points?

No, not on the part the credit covers. Chase's card page says purchases that qualify will not earn points.

Purchase Credit Earns points on
$300 flight booked with the airline $300 $0
$1,000 flight booked with the airline $300 $700

The card earns 8 points per dollar through Chase Travel and 4 points per dollar on flights and hotels booked direct. So a credit used on a direct flight gives up 4 points per dollar on that $300, or 1,200 points.

Where sources disagree

  • Posting time. Chase's guide says a credit may appear within a few days and can take one to two billing cycles to show on a statement. The Points Guy says a couple of days.
  • Refunds. One Mile at a Time says a refunded purchase should reverse the credit, which then applies to a later travel purchase, and that some readers report no reversal.

Not confirmed: whether Airbnb and other vacation rentals count, whether an authorized user's purchases draw on the same $300, and whether a Chase Travel booking uses this credit before the card's separate hotel credits.

The sums

Used in full, the credit brings the $795 fee down to $495. Whether the remaining credits cover that gap is a separate question, answered on the page about what the Reserve credits are really worth.

Who should skip counting on it

  • Anyone who spends under $300 a year on travel, parking, tolls and transit combined.
  • Anyone whose travel spending is mostly tours, attractions and theme park tickets. Those do not code as travel.
  • Anyone weighing the card on this credit alone. The Sapphire Preferred at $95 costs far less.

Quick answers

Does the Sapphire Reserve travel credit reset on January 1? No. It resets on the account anniversary year. The Points Guy reports that accounts opened before May 21, 2017 follow the calendar year.

Do I need to book through Chase Travel? No. Chase's guide says there is nothing to activate and no specific booking site to use.

Do parking and tolls count? Yes. Chase's travel category includes parking lots, garages, toll bridges and highways.
